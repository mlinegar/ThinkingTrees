\section{Information-Theoretic View of OPS}\label{sec:info-sieve}

This appendix records the narrow information-theoretic interpretation supported
by the current formalization. The right reading of OPS is \emph{oracle-sufficient
compression}, not generic lossless compression of raw text. The retained summary
need only preserve the distinctions relevant to the chosen oracle $\fstar$ and
to downstream objectives that factor through $\fstar$.

\begin{theorem}[Oracle sufficiency under the joint raw/summary law]\label{thm:info-suff-joint}
Fix a deterministic tree schedule $x \mapsto T(x)$, a stochastic summarizer $g$,
and a number of rounds $R \ge 1$. Let $X$ denote a raw document drawn from the
document law and let $Z$ denote the realized summary produced by the C-TreePO
pipeline on that document. If the local laws hold on every scheduled tree and
globally for $g$, then
\[
\fstar(Z) = \fstar(X) \qquad \text{almost surely.}
\]
Consequently there exists a measurable map $h$ such that
\[
\fstar(X) = h(Z) \qquad \text{almost surely.}
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
This is exactly the fixed-partition joint-law bridge formalized in Lean: local
laws imply zero oracle distortion on the support of the joint law, hence oracle
equality almost surely, and the almost-sure Doob--Dynkin factorization follows.
\end{proof}

Theorem~\ref{thm:info-suff-joint} is the formal justification for describing the
summary as \emph{oracle-sufficient}. It says that once the realized summary is
known, the raw document contains no additional information for the oracle task.
This is a Blackwell-style statement about task-relevant information, not a
source-coding statement about reconstructing the original text.

\begin{corollary}[Zero task-relevant KLIC]\label{cor:info-klic}
Suppose a downstream supervision density is oracle-indexed, meaning that for
some measurable $\bar p$,
\[
p(x,y) = \bar p(\fstar(x), y).
\]
Under the same joint-law assumptions as Theorem~\ref{thm:info-suff-joint},
\[
\mathrm{KL}\!\bigl(p(X,\cdot)\,\|\,p(Z,\cdot)\bigr) = 0
\qquad \text{almost surely.}
\]
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
If $\fstar(X)=\fstar(Z)$ almost surely and the density factors through
$\fstar$, then the two conditional densities agree pointwise almost surely; the
KL divergence therefore vanishes almost surely.
\end{proof}

The same bridge yields a score-transport statement for oracle-factorized
supervision: conditioning the raw-document score on the realized summary
preserves the oracle-indexed score under the joint law. This is the
information-theoretic sense in which C-TreePO preserves the downstream training
signal.

\paragraph{Anti-overclaim.}
Three scope limits are important.
\begin{enumerate}[nosep]
    \item The framework does \emph{not} promise reconstruction of the surface text.
    \item It does \emph{not} preserve objectives that depend on features outside the chosen oracle $\fstar$.
    \item If a downstream readout separates documents inside one oracle fiber, the guarantees no longer apply.
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{Relation to the Information Sieve.}
The analogy to the Information Sieve of \citet{steeg2016informationsieve} is
therefore heuristic rather than a full theorem-level identification. Both views
distinguish retained structure from discarded remainder, but our formalized
claim is only that the retained summary is sufficient for the supervised oracle
task. A fuller Shannon or mutual-information treatment can be added later in a
finite-support extension, but it is not required for the main theorem claims of
this paper.
