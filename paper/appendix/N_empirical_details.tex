\subsection{Mergeable Aggregation Validation Suite}\label{app:mergeable-validation}

This subsection consolidates our simulation-based validation of merge-safe aggregation under adaptive chunking.
The objective is to separate four effects that are often confounded in practice:
\begin{enumerate}[nosep]
    \item merge-rule correctness,
    \item sketch sufficiency (\(m\) versus \(k\)),
    \item chunk/selection quality under budget,
    \item confidence-interval calibration (coverage).
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{Why this matters.}
For non-additive targets (e.g., event-existence or threshold counts), naive repeated aggregation can be biased even when local predictions are good.
If chunking and retention are adaptive, severe undercoverage can arise even when point estimates look reasonable.
The merge-safe diagnostics below are designed to identify structural failure rather than only sampling noise.

\paragraph{Target family and setup.}
We evaluate targets of the form
\[
\theta_k \;=\; P(\texttt{count}\ge k \mid \texttt{spike}),
\]
where \(\texttt{count}\) is the number of threshold-exceeding local events in a document/sequence.
Each run chunks a sequence, retains a budgeted subset of chunks, merges kept chunks with a top-\(m\) sketch, and estimates \(\theta_k\).
The key theoretical regimes are:
\begin{itemize}[nosep]
    \item \(m<k\): unsupported (information-limited),
    \item \(m=k\): exact-supported,
    \item \(m>k\): over-supported.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Simulation families.}
The suite consists of:
\begin{enumerate}[nosep]
    \item \(m\times k\) phase sweeps with naive baselines,
    \item chunk-quality sweeps over leaf size and budget,
    \item nonlanguage scenario sweeps (ICU, intrusion, manufacturing, ECG),
    \item empirical CI-coverage sweeps (Wilson intervals per replicate).
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{Headline findings.}
\begin{enumerate}[nosep]
    \item \textbf{Sufficiency dominates:} unsupported \(m<k\) has large excess bias; over-supported \(m>k\) is close to \(m=k\).
    \item \textbf{Chunk quality and budget are distinct:} fine leaves alone do not guarantee recovery if budget is too small.
    \item \textbf{Not language-specific:} the same structural pattern appears in nonlanguage settings.
    \item \textbf{Coverage recovers with support:} low-budget undercoverage moves toward nominal as alignment and retained mass improve.
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{Complexity ladder and failure signatures.}
We also run a staged ladder that adds one modeling requirement at a time:
\(S1\) recover \(P(\texttt{spike})\), \(S2\) add \(P(\texttt{count}\ge2\mid\texttt{spike})\),
\(S3\) add \(P(\texttt{boundary}\mid\texttt{spike})\), \(S4\) add \(P(\texttt{count}\ge3\mid\texttt{spike})\),
and \(S5\) move to generic-\(k\) targets.
The positive controls remain stable (one-pass oracle \(0.0272\to0.0348\to0.0370\to0.0261\);
full model \(0.0311\to0.0389\to0.0335\to0.0268\), aggregate MAE across stages).
Failures are sharply component-specific:
\begin{enumerate}[nosep]
    \item \textbf{Missing boundary statistic:} in \(S4\), \texttt{full\_model\_missing\_boundary\_stat}
    has low error on non-boundary targets (\(\approx0.03\)--\(0.04\)) but boundary-target MAE \(=0.6500\).
    \item \textbf{Missing third-order statistic:} in \(S4\), \texttt{full\_model\_missing\_three\_stat}
    has non-3rd-target MAE \(\approx0.03\)--\(0.04\) but \(P(\texttt{count}\ge3\mid\texttt{spike})\) MAE \(=0.2997\).
    \item \textbf{Wrong chunker/selection:} \texttt{right\_rule\_wrong\_chunker} remains high-bias despite merge-safe rules
    (\(0.6085\) in \(S1\), \(0.4706\) in \(S2\), \(0.3902\) in \(S4\)).
    \item \textbf{Insufficient sketch order:} in \(S5\), \texttt{full\_model\_limited\_sketch} (top-3)
    is low-bias on supported targets (\(k=2:0.0373\), \(k=3:0.0257\)) and degrades once unsupported
    (\(k=4:0.0805\), \(k=5:0.1277\)).
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{Interpretation for deployment.}
The same decision rule emerges across all settings:
\begin{enumerate}[nosep]
    \item ensure sketch sufficiency (\(m\ge k\)),
    \item monitor capture diagnostics in addition to bias,
    \item treat low-budget undercoverage as structural failure,
    \item increase retained chunk budget/alignment before increasing model complexity.
\end{enumerate}
