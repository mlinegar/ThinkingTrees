\section{Measurement Error, Oracle Uncertainty, and Proxy-Aware Sampling}
\label{app:measurement-error}

This appendix isolates the two places where ``the oracle is imperfect'' can
enter the theory.
\begin{enumerate}[nosep]
    \item The oracle may be only a proxy for the true latent state we care
    about.
    \item The sampling or auditing design may depend on richer proxy
    information than the analyst uses in the IPW correction.
\end{enumerate}
The Lean development formalizes both effects and keeps them separate.

\subsection{Transport Error Plus Measurement Error}

Let $\Phi$ be a latent feature space with metric $d_{\Phi}$, let
$\phi:\Strings\to\Phi$ denote the true latent feature, and let
$\widehat{\phi}$ denote a possibly noisy measurement of that feature. We write
$Z$ for the reduced object produced by the summarization pipeline.

\begin{proposition}[Exact oracle identification]
\label{prop:exact-oracle-identification}
Suppose there exists a measurable map $h:\Y\to\Phi$ such that
$\phi(x)=h(\fstar(x))$ for every $x$. If the reduction is exact
theorem-backed, then every feature-indexed loss or utility transports exactly
through the reduction:
\[
\E\!\left[\ell\big(\phi(X)\big)\right]
=
\E\!\left[\ell\big(\phi(Z)\big)\right]
\]
for every measurable $\ell:\Phi\to\mathbb{R}$ for which the expectations exist.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Exact theorem-backedness gives $\fstar(Z)=\fstar(X)$ almost surely. Applying
$h$ to both sides yields $\phi(Z)=\phi(X)$ almost surely. Therefore
$\ell(\phi(Z))=\ell(\phi(X))$ almost surely, and the expectations are equal.
\end{proof}

The previous proposition is the ideal case. More realistically, the oracle may
control the latent feature only approximately.

\begin{proposition}[Approximate transport plus latent-state measurement error]
\label{prop:approx-transport-plus-measurement}
Assume:
\begin{enumerate}[nosep]
    \item the reduction is approximately theorem-backed with
    $\E[\metric(\fstar(Z),\fstar(X))]\le \Delta_{\mathrm{transport}}$;
    \item the latent feature is $K$-Lipschitz in oracle distance:
    \[
    d_{\Phi}\!\big(\phi(x),\phi(x')\big)\le
    K\,\metric\!\big(\fstar(x),\fstar(x')\big);
    \]
    \item the utility $u:\Phi\to\mathbb{R}$ is $1$-Lipschitz in $d_{\Phi}$.
\end{enumerate}
Then
\[
\left|
\E[u(\phi(X))] - \E[u(\widehat{\phi}(Z))]
\right|
\le
K\,\Delta_{\mathrm{transport}}
+
\E\!\left[d_{\Phi}\!\big(\phi(Z),\widehat{\phi}(Z)\big)\right].
\]
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
By the triangle inequality,
\[
\big|u(\phi(X)) - u(\widehat{\phi}(Z))\big|
\le
\big|u(\phi(X)) - u(\phi(Z))\big|
+
\big|u(\phi(Z)) - u(\widehat{\phi}(Z))\big|.
\]
Because $u$ is $1$-Lipschitz, the right-hand side is at most
\[
d_{\Phi}\!\big(\phi(X),\phi(Z)\big)
+
d_{\Phi}\!\big(\phi(Z),\widehat{\phi}(Z)\big).
\]
The feature Lipschitz assumption bounds the first term by
$K\,\metric(\fstar(X),\fstar(Z))$. Taking expectations gives
\[
\left|
\E[u(\phi(X))] - \E[u(\widehat{\phi}(Z))]
\right|
\le
K\,\E[\metric(\fstar(X),\fstar(Z))]
+
\E[d_{\Phi}(\phi(Z),\widehat{\phi}(Z))],
\]
and the assumed transport bound yields the claim.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{Interpretation.}
The first term is \emph{reduction error}: how much the tree changes the
oracle-indexed object. The second term is \emph{measurement error}: how much
the observed latent-state proxy differs from the latent state itself. Exact
oracle identification is simply the special case where the first term vanishes.

\subsection{Proxy-Aware Sampling and Nonclassical Error}

The sampling layer has a distinct problem. The inclusion law may depend on a
richer information set than the one used by the analyst in the IPW correction.
This is the nonclassical setting formalized in the Lean DSL layer.

Let $R\in\{0,1\}$ be the inclusion indicator, let $\pi_{\mathrm{true}}$ denote
the true inclusion probability under the actual design, and let
$\pi_{\mathrm{used}}$ denote the probability inserted into the analyst's IPW
weights. Define the design-adjusted quantity
\[
\widetilde Y
:=
Y_{\mathrm{pred}}
+
\frac{R}{\pi_{\mathrm{used}}}
\big(Y_{\mathrm{oracle}}-Y_{\mathrm{pred}}\big),
\]
where $Y_{\mathrm{true}}$ is the true target, $Y_{\mathrm{oracle}}$ is the
oracle-side proxy, and $Y_{\mathrm{pred}}$ is the model-based prediction used
for augmentation.

\begin{proposition}[Exact residual decomposition under proxy-aware sampling]
\label{prop:proxy-aware-residual}
Conditioning on the rich information set that determines the true design,
\[
\E[\widetilde Y - Y_{\mathrm{true}}]
=
\left(1-\frac{\pi_{\mathrm{true}}}{\pi_{\mathrm{used}}}\right)
\big(Y_{\mathrm{pred}}-Y_{\mathrm{oracle}}\big)
+
\big(Y_{\mathrm{oracle}}-Y_{\mathrm{true}}\big).
\]
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Using only $\E[R]=\pi_{\mathrm{true}}$ under the true design,
\begin{align*}
\E[\widetilde Y - Y_{\mathrm{true}}]
&=
\E\!\left[
Y_{\mathrm{pred}} - Y_{\mathrm{true}}
+
\frac{R}{\pi_{\mathrm{used}}}
\big(Y_{\mathrm{oracle}}-Y_{\mathrm{pred}}\big)
\right] \\
&=
\big(Y_{\mathrm{pred}} - Y_{\mathrm{true}}\big)
+
\frac{\pi_{\mathrm{true}}}{\pi_{\mathrm{used}}}
\big(Y_{\mathrm{oracle}}-Y_{\mathrm{pred}}\big).
\end{align*}
Rewrite the first term as
\[
\big(Y_{\mathrm{pred}} - Y_{\mathrm{true}}\big)
=
\big(Y_{\mathrm{oracle}} - Y_{\mathrm{true}}\big)
+
\big(Y_{\mathrm{pred}} - Y_{\mathrm{oracle}}\big),
\]
and combine the remaining two pieces:
\[
\big(Y_{\mathrm{pred}} - Y_{\mathrm{oracle}}\big)
+
\frac{\pi_{\mathrm{true}}}{\pi_{\mathrm{used}}}
\big(Y_{\mathrm{oracle}} - Y_{\mathrm{pred}}\big)
=
\left(1-\frac{\pi_{\mathrm{true}}}{\pi_{\mathrm{used}}}\right)
\big(Y_{\mathrm{pred}} - Y_{\mathrm{oracle}}\big).
\]
Substituting these identities back into the previous display gives the stated
formula.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Two clean special cases]
\label{cor:proxy-aware-special-cases}
From Proposition~\ref{prop:proxy-aware-residual}:
\begin{enumerate}[nosep]
    \item if $\pi_{\mathrm{used}}=\pi_{\mathrm{true}}$, only oracle
    measurement error remains;
    \item if $Y_{\mathrm{oracle}}=Y_{\mathrm{true}}$, only sampling-law
    mismatch remains;
    \item if both hold, the adjusted estimator is unbiased.
\end{enumerate}
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Each statement is obtained by setting the corresponding residual term in
Proposition~\ref{prop:proxy-aware-residual} to zero.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{What matters for IPW.}
The key point is that IPW does \emph{not} require classical measurement error.
It requires that the weighting model match the \emph{actual} inclusion law. If
the design is proxy-aware and the analyst adjusts the propensities to that
proxy-aware design, the Horvitz--Thompson cancellation still goes through. If
the analyst uses a coarser propensity, the residual term above is exactly the
bias that remains.

\subsection{How This Enters the Final Certificate}

Appendix~\ref{app:clustered-inference} showed how clustered confidence
intervals, calibration radii, and oracle-error envelopes enter the final
\texttt{computeDSLBound} object. The present appendix explains what the
oracle-error envelope itself means. It is not a mysterious extra term. It is
the measurement-error component in the decomposition
\[
\text{total decision error}
\le
\text{transport error}
+
\text{measurement error}
+
\text{sampling-law mismatch error}.
\]
In many deployments we simply assume the oracle is exact and set the middle
term to zero. The formalization does not require that assumption; it only makes
the exact-oracle case a particularly clean special case.
