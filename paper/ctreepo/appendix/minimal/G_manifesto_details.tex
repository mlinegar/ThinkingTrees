\section{Manifesto/Benoit Details}\label{app:min-manifesto}

\subsection{Measurement Target}

The Manifesto application uses party platforms and expert-survey means
from the Manifesto Project and \citet{BenoitEtAl2025}. The benchmark
contains $235$ party platforms matched to expert-survey estimates. Each
manifesto--dimension observation is scored on a seven-point policy scale.
Section~\ref{sec:min-manifesto-results} uses the economic dimension as the
running example; the headline table also reports the six-dimension macro.
The manifesto--dimension observation is the sampling unit: each item has
an observed root target, and the tree root prediction is evaluated against
that target.

\subsection{Pipeline}

The C-tree pipeline chunks each manifesto into contiguous character spans.
For the per-dimension variant, every leaf and merge prompt is conditioned
on one dimension's preservation rubric. The root summary is scored with
the corresponding Benoit rubric. The joint variant uses one union-rubric
summarizer and scores the resulting root summary six times. The headline
reports the root-observed corpus comparison; the node-level local-law
certificate is a separate, stronger claim tier (see \emph{Oracle-Grounded
Path} below).

\subsection{Main Reported Numbers}

At $8\mathrm{K}$-character leaves, the per-dimension tree reaches macro
Pearson $r=0.829$ against expert means, compared with $0.817$ for the
proprietary ensemble and $0.793$ for the matched Gemma-3 open-weight
replication. The economic row at the same leaf size is $r=0.939$. The
$4\mathrm{K}$ tiny-leaf extension reaches macro $0.842$ and economic $0.932$.
Across $4\mathrm{K}$--$64\mathrm{K}$ leaves, the macro spread is $0.027$.

The economic prompt-ladder pilot uses a token leaf-size axis
(Table~\ref{tab:min-manifesto-ladder}); the character-axis headline in
Section~\ref{sec:min-manifesto-results} is a separate comparison. Its
fully populated Benoit-init lane gives per-leaf best external Pearson
$0.830$ at $256$ tokens, $0.861$ at $512$, $0.885$ at $1024$, $0.886$
at $2048$, $0.886$ at $4096$, and $0.879$ at $8192$. The plateau from
$1024$ to $8192$ tokens is the evidence for leaf-size robustness under
prompt co-adaptation. Figure~\ref{fig:min-manifesto-ladder} shows the
full grid as a heatmap.

\subsection{Compute and Supervision Efficiency}\label{app:min-cost-comparison}

The C-tree pipeline runs on a single local GPU node with no proprietary
API access; the marginal compute cost is GPU electricity (a few cents
per manifesto at standard rates), and the full-corpus run reported in
Table~\ref{tab:min-benoit-headline} completed within a working day.

\paragraph{Call counts.} The per-manifesto LM call-count gap with
Benoit's published pipeline is roughly $6\times$. Benoit issues $18$
LM calls per manifesto--dimension across three proprietary frontier
models, totaling $108$ frontier-API calls per manifesto. The joint
C-tree variant on Gemma-4-31B-NVFP4 issues $2\lceil n_{\text{chars}}/c
\rceil - 1 + 6$ calls per manifesto (one summarize per leaf, one merge
per internal node, one score per dimension), or roughly $17$ calls for
$c = 8\mathrm{K}$.

\paragraph{Why supervision substitutes.} The contribution that
substitutes apples for apples is supervision efficiency. At a fixed
total supervision budget, cheap leaf-level supervision can stand in
for expensive root-level supervision without loss of performance. The
controlled Markov benchmark gives the cleanest evidence:
Figure~\ref{fig:min-markov-leaf-size} reports tree supervision (root
plus sampled leaf labels) matching or improving on flat root-only
supervision across the budget range, even down to $10\%$ root-label
coverage, with the gain depending mostly on the total number of
labeled units rather than on the root-vs-leaf split. The Manifesto
pipeline reads the same trade in the deployed direction: cheap
open-weight LM calls at every leaf and merge replace expensive
frontier-API calls at the root, the audit certifies the substitution
does not change the document-level estimand, and overall performance
hits parity with a much more expensive proprietary ensemble.

\subsection{Oracle-Grounded Path}

Expert quasi-sentence codings are available for $2{,}157$ platforms,
about $2.27$M coded spans. These annotations define a span oracle:
aggregate the quasi-sentence codes inside any leaf or internal-node span
and read out the corresponding economic score. This makes C1 and C3
explicit supervision signals; C2 remains a re-summarization drift probe.
These local-law training results are the stronger certificate path;
Table~\ref{tab:min-benoit-headline} is the root-observed comparison. The
two live on separate claim tiers.
