\subsection{Paper-to-Formalization Notes}

A few places in the paper use slightly different packaging than the
formalization does; these notes record the translation.

\paragraph{Law naming.} The canonical paper-facing law objects live in
\texttt{OPT/MergeTriangle.lean} under the paper's own vocabulary:
\texttt{LeafSufficiency} (C1), \texttt{RangeIdempotence} (C2),
\texttt{MergeSufficiency} and \texttt{SpanMergeSufficiency} (the first
link of C3, on realized merge inputs and on raw spans respectively),
\texttt{MergeTriangle} (the second link of C3,
Lemma~\ref{lem:merge-triangle}), and \texttt{ContextCompatible}
(Assumption~\ref{ass:context}). The older modules use a legacy numbering
L1/L2/L3 with the crosswalk
\[
\text{C1} \leftrightarrow \text{L1},\qquad
\text{C2} \leftrightarrow \text{L3},\qquad
\text{C3} \leftrightarrow \text{L2},
\]
kept only for compatibility; new statements cite the paper-named objects.

\paragraph{One-call laws vs legacy L2.} The two formalizations differ in
strength per node, and the difference matters for how the preservation
theorems should be read. The paper's C3 is a one-call law: at each merge,
one summarizer call on the (realized) concatenation is checked. The legacy
\texttt{L2} instead asserts zero expected distortion for the \emph{full
recursive reduction of the subtree} at each internal node,
$E[D(\mathrm{reduce}\,g\,\mathrm{node},\,S(\mathrm{node}))]=0$; at the root
that already is the conclusion of \texttt{one\_pass}, so the legacy
preservation theorems are read-offs of their hypotheses. The compositional
content of Theorem~\ref{thm:one-pass} is certified by the local route:
\texttt{one\_pass\_of\_local} proves root preservation by structural
induction from the one-call laws and \texttt{ContextCompatible}, and the
bridge \texttt{L2\_of\_local} derives the legacy L2 from those hypotheses,
so every legacy theorem downstream of L2 inherits a non-circular entry
point (\texttt{localLawsBundle\_of\_local}).

\paragraph{Support-form vs expectation-form laws.} The local-route laws are
stated support-wise (the property holds at every realization the summarizer
can produce), which matches the paper's ``$\sim$ almost surely'' reading and
avoids Lean's convention that a non-summable real series has \texttt{tsum}
zero. Consequences: the support-form theorems
(\texttt{one\_pass\_of\_local}, \texttt{multi\_round\_of\_local}) carry no
boundedness hypothesis, while the legacy expectation-form theorems
(\texttt{multi\_round\_proper} and its variants) require a bounded oracle
metric to rule out vacuously-satisfied expectation-zero hypotheses.

\paragraph{Literature formalization boundary.} The mergeable-sketch layer is
formalized at two levels. The algebraic/state layer is checked directly:
ordered list homomorphisms, Gray-style algebraic aggregate state, MUD tree
invariance, Feldman public-random seedwise streaming-to-general-MUD conversion,
Feldman exact seedwise success-probability bookkeeping, HLL max-register state
merge, Count-Min additive state merge, Misra-Gries executable
capacity/mass/positivity/decrement-debt accounting, SpaceSaving executable
capacity/mass bookkeeping, and the state-level sketch/readout reduction. The stricter coverage
pass also checks a Gibbons Section 5 \emph{stipulated} reference cost model
(the Big-O memberships are verified for reference growth functions assigned to
the three sorting routines; operation counts are not derived from the
algorithms), direct
ROLLUP-vs-CUBE update-count comparisons, Gray-style Boolean median/majority
finite-state lower bounds, HyperLogLog random ideal-hash seedwise algebra,
HyperLogLog `p=14' relative-standard-error arithmetic, and the HLL
Poisson-mixture series package plus its checked fixed-cardinality consequence
from a depoissonization-transfer obligation.
The Agarwal interval/range and epsilon-kernel interfaces now include checked
state-level tree-error transport theorems and exact all-points witnesses.  For
range queries, Lean verifies that exact all-points state has zero
\(\varepsilon n\) error for every nonnegative \(\varepsilon\), merges by
concatenation, and instantiates the generic mergeable query-sketch interface;
the finite trace layer additionally reuses mathlib's \texttt{Finset.Shatters},
\texttt{Finset.vcDim}, and Sauer--Shelah theorem to check the VC growth
bound for finite range spaces, and combines per-trace tails into a uniform
failure bound by a finite union bound.
For kernels, Lean verifies the common-reference-frame projection algebra:
max-projection state merges by optional \(\max\), min-projection state merges
by optional \(\min\), directional width is read from those merged extrema, and
directional width is invariant under translating all points by a common offset
and scales under nonnegative coordinate scaling.
The exact all-points
kernel has zero width error after arbitrary merge-tree evaluation.  The compact
range discrepancy and epsilon-kernel size constructions remain the external
randomized/geometric content of Agarwal's theorems.
For the hybrid quantile layer, Lean checks both the deterministic
promotion-monotonicity invariant and the finite-level random-buffer union bound
from per-level tail budgets.
For the same-weight interval construction, the Lean layer also checks the
deterministic core of Agarwal's random-halving analysis: the even/odd retained
halves give an unbiased two-point interval-count estimator, each one-step
over-count has absolute value at most one, the level-scaled over-count has
radius \(2^\ell\), and the complete-tree Hoeffding denominator and final
\(\varepsilon n\) scaling arithmetic are explicit. The complete-tree stochastic
step is also checked: Lean applies the reusable Azuma--Hoeffding theorem to an
adapted, bounded, conditional-mean-zero halving-error process and derives both
the two-sided root tail and the final \(\varepsilon n\) tail event.
The standalone entry point is
\texttt{FormalProbability.ML.MergeableSummaries.Literature}; the C-TreePO file
\texttt{OPT/MergeableReduction.lean} re-exports only the theorem names used by
the paper. Large external analytic or complexity facts are exposed as typed
theorem obligations. The external list, matching the repository's own
\texttt{Literature.externalObligationSurface}: HLL's remaining Mellin
estimates and analytic depoissonization proof; Agarwal's compact randomized
discrepancy and geometry constructions beyond the checked
union-bound/bookkeeping layers, together with the random-sample
\(\varepsilon\)-approximation tail and the randomized-quantile

Corollary~2 one-way mergeability (each carried as uninstantiated fields of
the corresponding algorithm bundles); the Misra--Gries Theorem~1 size and
frequency-error bounds (same mechanism; the executable bookkeeping layer is
checked); and Feldman's randomized/private-coin communication lower bounds,
including a deterministic linear Symmetric-Index bound and the Theorem~4/5
separation schemas. Names carrying the \texttt{\_statement} suffix in the
result map are Prop-valued citation schemas of this kind, not proved

Checked bridge lemmas then consume those typed obligations where the paper only
needs their algebraic consequence.

\paragraph{Bayes/mathlib backing.} The repo-specific Bayes layer remains
bounded, but it now uses mathlib where mathlib has the right object. Lean
aliases mathlib's event-conditioned Bayes theorem, conditional-probability
algebra and finite-fiber law of total probability, kernel/disintegration
posterior construction, posterior density/Radon--Nikodym formulas,
dominated-density/PDF facts, and \texttt{PMF} construction. The finite
learned-state posterior is packaged as a
mathlib \texttt{PMF} under nonnegative-prior, nonnegative-likelihood, and
positive-evidence assumptions, with singleton and arbitrary-event masses of
the induced measure identified explicitly.  The finite Bayes layer also checks
that positive normalization preserves MAP decisions, posterior odds cancel the
evidence, and posterior expectations/fixed posterior functionals factor
through the learned state whenever the likelihood does. It also checks the
corresponding finite posterior-predictive readout over a fixed future
observation; when the likelihood
factors through the learned state, the raw and state finite Bayes \texttt{PMF}s
are equal. The
posterior-consistency predicates used by the paper are proved definitionally
equal to mathlib \texttt{TendstoInMeasure}. This
formalizes Bayes alignment and exact state/readout transport, not posterior
calibration, continuous learned-state Bayes, SSNL flow-density/Jacobian
semantics, or classical posterior consistency from primitive regularity
conditions.

\paragraph{Distortion symbols.} The formalization distinguishes the
document-level $\Delta_R$ from the generic audited-population means
$\mu_{\mathrm{dist}}^\star,\mu_{\mathrm{dist}}^J$ that appear in the
envelope algebra of \S\ref{sec:estimation}. The two coincide only under
an explicit unit/coupling identification; the paper follows the same
distinction.
