\section{Quasi-Sentence Leaf-Size Ladder: Full Grids}\label{app:qsentence-leaf-ladder}

Section~\ref{sec:qsentence-results} reports the abridged leaf-size
ladder for the neural-operator family. This appendix gives the full
grids for both families on the shared bundles of
Section~\ref{sec:qsentence-harness}, the protocol details needed to
reproduce them, and the expert-dimension grid with its winning
hyperparameters.

\subsection{Protocol}

All runs consume the frozen labeled-tree bundles: $218$ Western
European platforms, split $140/30/48$ (train/validation/test) shared
across leaf sizes $\ell \in \{1,2,4,8,16\}$ quasi-sentences, every node
stamped with its gold compact state
$(\mathrm{RILE}_{\mathrm{norm}}, \mathrm{dom}_1,\dots,\mathrm{dom}_7)$
by additive CMP-count aggregation (no LLM labeling). The alternating
schedule is two iterations, $f$ first ($f^1g^1 \to f^2g^1 \to f^2g^2$),
so the $f$-stage row isolates the readout trained on leaf states with
the merge untrained, and the $g$-stage row adds the learned merge
trained against the current $f$.

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.5em]
    \item \textbf{Neural-operator family.} Leaves embedded once by
          \textsc{embeddinggemma-300m}; $f$ and $g$ are FNO stacks
          ($8$ epochs, batch $16$, learning rate $3\times 10^{-3}$)
          trained on node-level losses. Embeddings are computed once
          per bundle and disk-cached; the FNO training itself is
          seconds per cell.
    \item \textbf{LLM prompt-program family.} $f$ and $g$ are prompt
          programs optimized by GEPA (light budget) against node-level
          accuracy rewards, over a fixed open-weight checkpoint; the
          two runtimes reported are Gemma-4-31B-IT-NVFP4 and a
          DiffusionGemma fleet.
\end{itemize}

Internal metrics compare root predictions to the teacher rollup (gold
compact state of the root); external metrics compare to the published
MPDS document score. Both are on the normalized $[0,1]$ scale.

Three readings. First, the $\ell = 8$ peak with a genuine $f\to g$
composition lift ($0.664 \to 0.718$ external) is the ladder's
headline; the learned merge is doing work there, and the internal and
external columns agree to within $0.005$ at every cell, which is the
rollup-validity result of Section~\ref{sec:qsentence-labels} showing up
as a consistency check. Second, the profile is non-monotone in $\ell$:
$\ell = 1$ maximizes the number of supervised nodes (about $437$K) but
also maximizes merge-chain depth for a trained operator, and at this
fixed training budget the trade resolves at intermediate granularity.
Third, at $\ell = 2$ the $g$-stage sits slightly below the $f$-stage
($0.537$ vs.\ $0.553$), the one cell where training the merge does not
pay; the MAE column shows the same cell as the grid's worst, so the
effect is a training artifact at the deepest useful chain length rather
than measurement noise.

The two rows per leaf tell two separate cautionary stories, both
methodological rather than substrate-specific. The $f$-stage internal
$r = 1.000$ with MAE $0.000$ is exact: the readout prompt sees the gold
leaf label in its trace and copies it. Any pipeline whose leaf stage
can see its own supervision will produce this row, and it looks like a
perfect model until the label is withheld. The $g$-stage collapse
(internal $r \in [-0.095, 0.026]$ at $\ell \geq 4$) has the objective-
mismatch diagnosis given in Section~\ref{sec:qsentence-results}: the
prompt-search reward is a mean-over-dimensions accuracy, and
predicting each dimension's corpus mean is a degenerate optimum worth
about $0.90$ of that reward. Neither failure is an argument that a
language model cannot implement the merge; both are arguments that the
objective must price within-dimension fidelity (the local laws) and
that leaf supervision must be hidden from the stage being evaluated.
The in-framework fix exists: the $g$-stage objective accepts local-law
reward terms through the ladder flags
\texttt{--dspy-g-law-c1-reward-weight},
\texttt{--dspy-g-law-c3a-reward-weight}, and
\texttt{--dspy-g-law-c3b-reward-weight}, which price the local laws
directly in the prompt-search reward (a within-dimension fidelity
reward is the narrower alternative). All three weights default to
zero, and no run has yet set them nonzero. The collapse rows are
therefore reported as what they are: the behavior of the zero-weight
objective. The law-weighted run is scoped follow-up work, and until it
lands the LLM column reads as an objective failure, not a substrate
limit.

\subsection{Expert-Dimension Grid and Winning Hyperparameters}

The expert-dimension reconstruction of
Section~\ref{sec:qsentence-results} uses the neural-operator family at
$\ell = 16$ with LLM-generated span supervision at internal nodes,
scored against the \citet{BenoitEtAl2025} expert means on the test
split, three seeds per dimension. Hyperparameters were selected by an
HPO sweep on the economic dimension only
(best configuration: $384$ Fourier modes, hidden width $32$, two
layers, head width $256$, $12$ epochs, learning rate
$2.4\times10^{-3}$, loss weights leaf $1.57$ / merge $0.89$ / root
$3.08$) and transferred to the other five dimensions with no
per-dimension tuning.

\begin{table}[htbp]
    \centering
    \begin{tabular}{lcccc}
    \toprule

    economic         & $0.661 \pm 0.045$ & $0.633$ & $50$ & flat \\

    \bottomrule

    \caption{Neural-operator family vs.\ Benoit expert-survey means,

The ordering in Table~\ref{tab:app-benoit-6dim-fno} matches the
CMP-code construct ceiling of Section~\ref{sec:qsentence-labels}
(cross-validated $R^2$ from code counts: decentralization $0.03$, EU
$0.18$, up to economic $\sim 0.40$), with one caveat each way:
immigration's mean is soft (seed spread $0.31$--$0.52$), and
decentralization has full expert coverage yet still floors, so the
floor is signal, not sample size. Per-dimension HPO studies exist for
the four weaker dimensions (about $50$ Optuna trials each; one
\texttt{best.json} per dimension under
\texttt{outputs/hpo\_*\_chunkfno/}), with best validation objectives of
$0.554$ for immigration, $0.615$ for environment, $0.540$ for EU, and
$0.679$ for decentralization; social, already the strongest dimension
under the transferred recipe, has no per-dimension study. These are
validation objectives, not test results, and the decentralization gap
($0.679$ validation against the $0.244$ transferred-recipe test $r$) is
wide enough that validation overfitting is plausible.
Table~\ref{tab:app-benoit-6dim-fno} therefore reports the transferred
economic recipe as the result of record; a per-dimension-tuned
three-seed test reconstruction is scoped follow-up work and slots into
the same table when it lands.
