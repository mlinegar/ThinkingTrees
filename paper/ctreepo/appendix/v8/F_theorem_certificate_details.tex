\section{Theorem and Certificate Details}
\label{app:v8-theorem-certificate-details}
\label{app:v8-full-proofs}

This appendix records the proof details behind the v8 theorem ladder and
finite-sample certificate. The Lean crosswalk in
Appendix~\ref{app:v8-lean-crosswalk} lists machine-checked surfaces; the
purpose here is to state the paper-facing mathematical argument in one place.

\subsection{Setup and Notation}

Fix a realized C-Tree \(T\) for a manifesto \(x\). Each node \(v\) represents
a contiguous raw span \(x_v\) and stores a produced policy-evidence state
\(z_v\). Write
\[
  a\sim_\ast b
  \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
  d_Y\!\left(f^\ast(a),f^\ast(b)\right)=0 .
\]
The exact local laws say:
\[
\begin{array}{ll}
\mathrm{C1:} & g(x_v)\sim_\ast x_v \quad\text{for leaves},\\[2mm]
\mathrm{C2:} & g(z_v)\sim_\ast z_v \quad\text{for produced states},\\[2mm]
\mathrm{C3:} & g(z_u,z_w)\sim_\ast x_u\concat x_w
  \quad\text{for sibling nodes }u,w .
\end{array}
\]
The displayed form suppresses implementation details about serialization. The
state \(g(z_u,z_w)\) is the parent state produced from the two child states,
and \(x_u\concat x_w\) is the represented parent manifesto span.

\subsection{Root Preservation}

\begin{proposition}[Exact local laws preserve every represented span]
\label{prop:v8-all-node-preservation}
If C1 and C3 hold on the realized tree \(T\), then every produced node state
\(z_v\) satisfies \(z_v\sim_\ast x_v\). In particular, the root state
preserves the full manifesto for \(f^\ast\).
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Proceed by induction over the tree. At a leaf, \(z_v=g(x_v)\), so C1 gives
\(z_v\sim_\ast x_v\). At an internal node \(v\) with children \(u,w\), the
parent state is \(z_v=g(z_u,z_w)\). C3 states directly that this merge state
is \(f^\ast\)-equivalent to the represented parent span
\(x_u\concat x_w=x_v\). Thus the claim holds for every internal node, and the
root case is the full manifesto span.
\end{proof}

The proof above is intentionally minimal because C3 is stated at the
represented-span level. If C3 is instead stated as compatibility of child
equivalences with the merge state, the usual induction hypothesis supplies the
child-span equivalences and the same conclusion follows by transitivity of
\(\sim_\ast\). C2 is not needed for this one fresh bottom-up pass. It is needed
when produced states are re-entered into the state map: cleanup passes,
stochastic re-summarization, repeated reductions, and audit probes all feed an
already-produced state back through \(g\).

\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:v8-root-preservation}]
Theorem~\ref{thm:v8-root-preservation} is the root instance of
Proposition~\ref{prop:v8-all-node-preservation}. Applying the definition of
\(\sim_\ast\) at the root gives
\[
  d_Y\!\left(f^\ast(z_{\mathrm{root}}),f^\ast(x)\right)=0 .
\]
\end{proof}

\subsection{Fixed Deterministic Partitions}
\label{app:v8-fixed-partition}

\begin{proposition}[Extension to any deterministic fixed partition]
\label{prop:v8-fixed-partition}
Let \(\Pi\) be any deterministic rule that maps a manifesto \(x\) to a finite
ordered rooted tree \(T_\Pi(x)\) with contiguous leaves. If C1/C2/C3 hold on
the realized calls induced by \(T_\Pi(x)\), then the root state of that tree
preserves \(x\) for \(f^\ast\). Consequently the statement holds after
averaging over any distribution of manifestos.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Condition on \(x\). Since \(\Pi\) is deterministic, \(T_\Pi(x)\) is fixed.
The root-preservation proof applies to this realized tree and yields zero
root distortion conditional on \(x\). Taking expectation over \(X\) gives the
population statement. The proposition is conditional: it does not say that an
arbitrary partition rule automatically satisfies the local laws; it says that
the local-to-global implication is independent of which deterministic
contiguous partition was chosen.
\end{proof}

\subsection{C2 Is Independent}
\label{app:v8-c2-counterexample}

The following construction shows why re-entry preservation is a separate
local law.

\begin{example}[C1 and C3 do not imply C2]
\label{ex:v8-c2-independent}
Let raw strings be finite sequences over two distinguished tokens
\(\{\mathrm{D},\mathrm{C}\}\). Define \(f^\ast(x)=1\) when the first token of
non-empty \(x\) is \(\mathrm{D}\), and \(f^\ast(x)=0\) otherwise. Interpret
\(\mathrm{D}\) as a devolution cue and \(\mathrm{C}\) as a centralization cue.

Define a state map \(g_{\mathrm{bad}}\) by
\[
g_{\mathrm{bad}}(x)=
\begin{cases}
\mathrm{C}, & x=\mathrm{D}\text{ or }x=\mathrm{C},\\
\mathrm{D}, & x\notin\{\mathrm{D},\mathrm{C}\}\text{ and }f^\ast(x)=1,\\
\mathrm{C}, & \text{otherwise.}
\end{cases}
\]
On fresh raw inputs, \(g_{\mathrm{bad}}\) emits the token with the same oracle
value as the input, so C1 holds. On fresh sibling inputs, the merged
representation begins with the left child's emitted token and therefore
preserves the first-token oracle for the concatenated span, so C3 holds on
fresh merges. But \(\mathrm{D}\) lies in the range of \(g_{\mathrm{bad}}\) and
\(g_{\mathrm{bad}}(\mathrm{D})=\mathrm{C}\), so re-summarizing the produced
state flips the oracle value. Hence C2 fails.
\end{example}

In manifesto terms, a system can be faithful when it first compresses a raw
span and still drift when it is asked to compress its own policy state again.
C2 rules out that drift.

\subsection{Oracle-Compatible Readouts and Preferences}

A readout \(\Phi\) is oracle-compatible when it is constant on
\(f^\ast\)-fibers:
\[
  a\sim_\ast b \Rightarrow \Phi(a)=\Phi(b).
\]
By Proposition~\ref{prop:v8-all-node-preservation}, every valid node state can
replace its represented raw span for such a readout. At the root, the C-Tree
state can replace the full manifesto. At internal nodes, the same statement is
the justification for local supervision and audit: a valid state is a valid
measurement object for the span it represents.

Pairwise preference transfer is the two-argument version. If a comparison rule
\(P\) is constant on oracle fibers in both arguments, then
\[
  P(z_v,z_{v'})=P(x_v,x_{v'})
\]
for valid realized node states. A DPO or GRPO application therefore requires
more than a valid C-Tree: the preference input channel must be compatible with
the manifesto policy oracle being preserved. Preferences over style, phrasing,
or formatting are outside this claim unless the oracle is widened to include
those features.

\begin{proof}[Proof of Proposition~\ref{prop:v8-manifesto-preference-equivalence}]
Let \(E_\theta(x)\) be the population integrand for the downstream preference
method at parameter \(\theta\). Oracle compatibility means that there exists a
function \(\phi_\theta\) such that
\[
  E_\theta(x)=\phi_\theta(f^\ast(x)).
\]
For a valid C-Tree root \(z_{\mathrm{root}}\), Theorem~\ref{thm:v8-root-preservation}
gives \(f^\ast(z_{\mathrm{root}})=f^\ast(x)\). Therefore
\[
  E_\theta(z_{\mathrm{root}})
  =
  \phi_\theta(f^\ast(z_{\mathrm{root}}))
  =
  \phi_\theta(f^\ast(x))
  =
  E_\theta(x)
\]
pointwise for every manifesto. Averaging over the manifesto population gives
equality of the population preference objective for every \(\theta\), and
therefore the same optimizer set whenever the optimizer exists.
\end{proof}

\subsection{DPO and GRPO Transport Conditions}

The exact preference result above is qualitative. Approximate preservation
requires a transport condition that converts oracle distortion into objective
drift. For DPO, one sufficient condition is Lipschitz dependence of policy
log-ratios on the manifesto policy oracle.

\begin{lemma}[DPO pointwise transport]
\label{lem:v8-dpo-transport}
For a preferred--dispreferred pair \((a_w,a_l)\), define the DPO pointwise
loss
\[
\ell_{\mathrm{DPO}}(x,a_w,a_l)
=
-\log\sigma\!\left(
\beta\left[
\log\frac{\pi_\theta(a_w\mid x)}{\pi_{\mathrm{ref}}(a_w\mid x)}
-
\log\frac{\pi_\theta(a_l\mid x)}{\pi_{\mathrm{ref}}(a_l\mid x)}
\right]\right).
\]
If each policy log-ratio is \(L_{\mathrm{pol}}\)-Lipschitz in
\(d_Y(f^\ast(x),f^\ast(x'))\), then
\[
|\ell_{\mathrm{DPO}}(x,a_w,a_l)-\ell_{\mathrm{DPO}}(x',a_w,a_l)|
\le
2|\beta|L_{\mathrm{pol}}\,
d_Y(f^\ast(x),f^\ast(x')) .
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Let \(\Delta(x)\) be the bracketed log-ratio difference. The two log-ratio
terms each move by at most
\(L_{\mathrm{pol}}d_Y(f^\ast(x),f^\ast(x'))\), so
\[
  |\Delta(x)-\Delta(x')|
  \le
  2L_{\mathrm{pol}}d_Y(f^\ast(x),f^\ast(x')) .
\]
The function \(-\log\sigma(t)\) is 1-Lipschitz because its derivative has
absolute value \(1-\sigma(t)\le 1\). Scaling by \(|\beta|\) gives the stated
constant.
\end{proof}

For a DPO population objective, the pair generator must also be
oracle-compatible: replacing \(x\) by another input with the same preserved
manifesto policy oracle must not change the distribution of comparison pairs.
Under that premise, Lemma~\ref{lem:v8-dpo-transport} lifts from pointwise
pairs to the expected DPO integrand with the same transport constant. For
GRPO-style group ranking or reinforcement objectives, v8 makes the analogous
assumption directly: the expected group loss, including ranking, reward,
clipping, and KL terms, is Lipschitz in the preserved manifesto policy oracle.
These are assumptions on the downstream preference channel, not consequences
of tree compression alone.

\subsection{Population Gap Bound}

\begin{proposition}[Population transport]
\label{prop:v8-population-transport}
Suppose the downstream method has an expected loss integrand \(E_\theta\)
satisfying
\[
  |E_\theta(a)-E_\theta(b)|
  \le
  C_{\mathrm{meth}}d_Y(f^\ast(a),f^\ast(b)).
\]
Let \(\Delta_R\) be the population mean distortion between each produced state
and its represented raw span. Then the downstream objective drift satisfies
\[
  |G_{\mathrm{meth}}|
  \le
  C_{\mathrm{meth}}\Delta_R .
\]
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
For each represented unit, apply the transport inequality to the raw span and
the produced state. Average over the finite node population or over the
manifesto distribution, depending on the target population. The left side is
the absolute difference between the downstream objective evaluated on raw
inputs and on produced states; the right side is
\(C_{\mathrm{meth}}\) times the mean oracle distortion. This is exactly the
definition of \(\Delta_R\).
\end{proof}

Exact C1/C2/C3 laws give \(\Delta_R=0\) on the relevant realized population.
Approximate laws produce a nonzero budget; the audit estimates that budget.

\subsection{Horvitz--Thompson Unbiasedness}

Let \(\mathcal U\) be the finite population of sample-eligible tree units,
\(N=|\mathcal U|\), and \(D_i\ge 0\) the distortion contribution for unit
\(i\). The target finite-population mean is
\[
  \Delta=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i\in\mathcal U}D_i .
\]
Let \(Z_i\) be the inclusion indicator and
\(\pi_i=\mathbb P(Z_i=1)\) its logged marginal inclusion probability, with
\(0<\pi_i\le 1\). The Horvitz--Thompson estimator is
\[
  \widehat{\Delta}_{\mathrm{HT}}
  =
  \frac{1}{N}\sum_{i\in\mathcal U}\frac{Z_i}{\pi_i}D_i .
\]
Taking expectation under the sampling design,
\[
  \mathbb E[\widehat{\Delta}_{\mathrm{HT}}]
  =
  \frac{1}{N}\sum_{i\in\mathcal U}
  \frac{\mathbb E[Z_i]}{\pi_i}D_i
  =
  \Delta .
\]
This proof uses only logged marginal propensities and positivity. Independence
is not required for unbiasedness. Independence, negative dependence, or
bounded covariance assumptions enter only when reporting variance or
high-probability envelopes.

\subsection{Proxy-Corrected Local-Law Loss}

For a node \(v\), let \(\widehat \ell_v\) be the dense proxy law loss and
\(\ell_v^\ast\) the target-oracle law loss. With audit indicator \(R_v\) and
logged propensity \(\pi_v>0\), v8 uses
\[
  \ell_v^{\mathrm{corr}}
  =
  \widehat\ell_v
  +
  \frac{R_v}{\pi_v}
  (\ell_v^\ast-\widehat\ell_v).
\]
Conditioning on the realized tree and proxy values,
\[
\mathbb E[\ell_v^{\mathrm{corr}}\mid T]
=
\widehat\ell_v
+
\frac{\mathbb E[R_v\mid T]}{\pi_v}
(\ell_v^\ast-\widehat\ell_v)
=
\ell_v^\ast .
\]
Summing with the declared depth weights gives an unbiased estimate of the
oracle local-law channel. The correction changes the estimator, not the
population target.

\subsection{Certificate Decomposition}

The deployed certificate may use an accessible judge \(J\) for audited
distortion. Let \(\mu^\ast\) be the true oracle mean distortion,
\(\mu^J\) the corresponding accessible-judge mean, and
\(\widehat\Delta_{\mathrm{HT}}^J\) the HT estimate. Let
\(\widehat\Delta_{\mathrm{HT}}\) denote the reported estimate after any
clipping or residual truncation. Suppose the following envelope inequalities
hold on an event \(\mathcal E\):
\[
\begin{aligned}
C_{\mathrm{meth}}|\mu^\ast-\mu^J| &\le B_{\mathrm{cal}},\\
C_{\mathrm{meth}}|\mu^J-\widehat\Delta_{\mathrm{HT}}^J|
&\le B_{\mathrm{est}},\\
C_{\mathrm{meth}}|\widehat\Delta_{\mathrm{HT}}^J
-\widehat\Delta_{\mathrm{HT}}|
&\le B_{\mathrm{clip}} .
\end{aligned}
\]
Combining Proposition~\ref{prop:v8-population-transport} with a telescoping
decomposition gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
|G_{\mathrm{meth}}|
&\le C_{\mathrm{meth}}\mu^\ast\\
&\le
C_{\mathrm{meth}}\widehat\Delta_{\mathrm{HT}}
+
C_{\mathrm{meth}}|\mu^\ast-\mu^J|
+
C_{\mathrm{meth}}|\mu^J-\widehat\Delta_{\mathrm{HT}}^J|
+
C_{\mathrm{meth}}|\widehat\Delta_{\mathrm{HT}}^J
-\widehat\Delta_{\mathrm{HT}}|\\
&\le
C_{\mathrm{meth}}\widehat\Delta_{\mathrm{HT}}
+
B_{\mathrm{cal}}
+
B_{\mathrm{est}}
+
B_{\mathrm{clip}} .
\end{aligned}
\]
If \(\mathbb P(\mathcal E)\ge 1-\delta\), the displayed certificate holds
with probability at least \(1-\delta\). In practice, \(\mathcal E\) is the
intersection of calibration, sampling, and clipping events, and the reported
failure probability follows by a union bound.

\subsection{Propensity and Clipping Robustness}

The HT guarantee is robust to adaptive or adversarial sampling designs in the
following limited sense: unbiasedness is unchanged as long as the logged
marginal inclusion probabilities are correct and strictly positive. A hostile
or highly unequal design can still increase variance. A practical stress
class is
\[
  \mathcal D(\pi_{\min},\kappa)
  =
  \{\pi:\ \pi_i\in[\pi_{\min},\kappa\pi_{\min}],
  \ \sum_i\pi_i=n\}.
\]
Within this class, small \(\pi_{\min}\) and large spread \(\kappa\) inflate
the worst-case variance but do not change the finite-population estimand. If
distortions are bounded by \(D_{\max}\), a Bernoulli-style variance proxy is
\[
  \operatorname{Var}(\widehat\Delta_{\mathrm{HT}})
  \le
  \frac{D_{\max}^2}{N}
  \left(\frac{1}{\pi_{\min}}-1\right),
\]
up to the covariance correction for the actual design. Clipping the inverse
propensity weights at \(w_{\max}\) trades variance for bias; that bias is the
\(B_{\mathrm{clip}}\) term in the certificate rather than an unreported
change in target.

For Manifesto measurement, the design consequence is simple. A node-level
certificate should report the sampling law, the logged propensities, the
effective sample size or variance envelope, the clipping rule, and diagnostic
slices by policy dimension. Decentralization should be an explicit slice
because the root and teacher-trace evidence identify it as the main local
failure mode.
