\title{C-TreePO: Learned Mergeable Sketches for Design-Based Long-Document Measurement}
\author{Mitchell Linegar}
\date{}

\begin{document}
\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
Large language models make it cheap to score long political documents, but the
object being scored is often a black-box compression: a chunked, retrieved, or
recursively summarized representation rather than the document itself. The
statistical question is whether that representation is a sufficient statistic
for the target oracle. C-TreePO frames long-document compression as a learned
mergeable sketch. A summarizer $g$ maps spans into observable text states, a
readout $f$ scores the root, and local laws ask whether leaf summaries,
re-summary steps, and merges preserve the oracle along the realized tree. This
keeps the state-merge-readout discipline of classical sketches while replacing
hand-designed registers with task-relative LLM summaries that can be read and
audited. Design-Based Supervised Learning (DSL) supplies the measurement
design: root labels anchor the full-document target; randomly sampled leaf and
merge labels audit and train the compression path; logged propensities let
IPW/Horvitz--Thompson estimators recover tree-wide distortion. Under exact
local laws, the root preserves the full-document oracle; under approximate
laws, node audits yield a certificate for compression-induced distortion. The
main application is Manifesto/Benoit political-text measurement. On six policy
dimensions, a single open-weight C-Tree pipeline reaches macro Pearson
$r=0.829$ against expert-survey means, above Benoit's proprietary 18-score
ensemble ($0.817$) and matched open-weight replication ($0.793$). Economic
policy shows the supervision tradeoff most clearly: root accuracy is stable
across character leaf sizes, and an alternating prompt ladder gives a
$0.007$-wide plateau across $1024$--$8192$ token leaves. Controlled
HyperLogLog, private mergeable-sketch, Markov, and neural-operator results
provide appendix-backed validation anchors. The contribution is a
political-methods account of when LLM compression is an interpretable learned
sketch that can be audited, certified, and used for downstream measurement.
\end{abstract}
