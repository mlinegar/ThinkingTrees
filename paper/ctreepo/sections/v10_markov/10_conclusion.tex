\section{Conclusion}\label{sec:v8-conclusion}

C-TreePO treats long-document measurement as a state-composition
problem. The expert score is global; the evidence that determines it
is local and relational. A C-Tree stores policy-evidence states at
the leaves and at every internal merge, and the local laws say when
those states can replace the raw spans they represent. The objective
separates two channels: a root loss fits the document-level expert
target, and a local-law channel trains and audits the state map
inside the realized tree. Sparse oracle calls and logged
propensities correct proxy bias in the local channel.

The Manifesto/Benoit results give us the root-observed tier. The
per-dimension tree reaches macro \(r=0.829\); the universal-summary
configuration reaches \(0.807\). Five of the six policy dimensions
track their per-dimension ceilings under shared compression.
Decentralization is where shared compression breaks down, and the
local diagnostic gap flags that breakdown directly, without any
appeal to root-score comparisons. The Markov DGP runs in
Section~\ref{sec:v10-markov-results} repeat the same shape on a
controlled benchmark with a constructible oracle: where the DGP
requires cross-leaf interactions, multi-leaf trees with the
local-law package cut flat-FNO root MAE by 0.26 (0.182 versus
0.440); where local information is enough, both architectures hit
the same recovery floor.

The certificate of Section~\ref{sec:v8-audit-certificate} closes the
loop. Sampled oracle calls with logged propensities give an unbiased
estimate of representation distortion
(Proposition~\ref{prop:v10-ht-unbiased}), and the four-term
decomposition
(Proposition~\ref{prop:v10-finite-sample-certificate}) attributes
remaining drift to the components an analyst can act on.
Manifesto Project quasi-sentences are the natural sampling frame for
the next-tier human-graded audit, and the same machinery applies
without further development of the framework.
