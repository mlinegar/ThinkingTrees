\section{Preference Training, Distillation, and Scope}\label{sec:min-applications}

The practical training path is a supervision cascade. At the top, gold root
labels or trusted expert measurements calibrate the full-document target
oracle. A high-quality proxy judge, such as a deliberation-grade LLM or
human-in-the-loop workflow, can then score more nodes than the gold process can
afford. Local-law audits and quasi-sentence-style span labels turn those node
judgments into supervision for cheaper summarizers and scorers. Repeating the
process distills the compression tree downward: each cheaper stage is useful
only insofar as it continues to preserve the same task state measured by the
stronger stage.

For preference optimization, the extra premise is oracle factorization. If a
preference, reward, or loss depends on a manifesto only through the preserved
oracle value, then DPO, GRPO, and related objectives can be trained on root
summaries without changing the population target. If preferences also depend
on tone, persuasion, writing style, or other surface features not included in
the oracle, the premise fails. The state must widen, or the certificate must
report the remaining residual.

The same caution applies to the node/root supervision tradeoff. Local labels
shrink estimation uncertainty when they measure the same preservation property
as the root task. Judge calibration remains a separate problem. In the
certificate, additional local labels reduce $B_{\mathrm{est}}$; root labels
and held-out calibration checks identify $B_{\mathrm{cal}}$, the gap between
the accessible judge and the trusted social-science target.

C-TreePO is therefore best understood as a methods layer for long, sectioned,
rubric-driven documents: manifestos, policy platforms, legal records,
clinical narratives, and book-length reports. It is useful when a domain can
name a task-relative state, a readout, and local checks that preserve that
state. The method makes the preservation claim observable: root accuracy,
local-law distortion, calibration error, and sampling uncertainty are separate
objects with separate evidence.
