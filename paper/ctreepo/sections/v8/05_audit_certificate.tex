\section{Audit and Proxy-Corrected Estimation}
\label{sec:v8-audit-certificate}

\subsection{Audit Units}

The audit is a finite-population survey over a realized manifesto tree. For a
document \(x\), the population contains the leaf states, produced states, and
merge states whose C1/C2/C3 losses define the local-law channel. Each audit
unit \(i\) has a represented raw span \(S(i)\), a produced state \(s_i\), a
law channel, and a logged marginal inclusion probability \(\pi_i>0\).

The first diagnostic quantity is a violation rate for each law channel. It
tells the analyst whether the problem is leaf retention, re-summary drift, or
merge failure. The certificate quantity is mean distortion. A run can have few
violations with large distortion on the failing nodes, or many small
violations whose root impact is limited. Both diagnostics should be reported
when the node-level audit is run.

\subsection{Dense Proxy, Sparse Oracle}

The statistical problem is that \(L_{\mathrm{law}}^\ast\) in
Equation~\eqref{eq:v8-local-law-estimand} is usually not fully observed. In
the Manifesto application, root expert-survey means are observed for held-out
documents, while local checks are usually available through teacher traces,
quasi-sentence-derived targets, or a learned readout \(\widehat f\). Direct
local calls to the target oracle \(f^\ast\) are sparse.

The dense local quantity is the same C1/C2/C3 comparison measured through the
fitted proxy:
\[
  \widehat\ell_v
  =
  \ell_{\mathrm{law}}(v;\widehat f,g).
\]
The proxy gives training signal throughout the tree. It is useful only because
sampled oracle nodes can estimate how the proxy surface differs from the
target oracle surface. Define the node bias
\[
  b_v=\widehat\ell_v-\ell_v^\ast .
\]

Let \(R_v\in\{0,1\}\) indicate whether node \(v\) is sampled and evaluated
with \(f^\ast\), and let \(\pi_v>0\) be the logged marginal probability that
the audit design samples \(v\). The corrected node loss is
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:v8-corrected-node-loss}
  \ell_v^{\mathrm{corr}}
  =
  \widehat\ell_v
  +
  \frac{R_v}{\pi_v}
  \left(\ell_v^\ast-\widehat\ell_v\right).
\end{equation}
Unsampled nodes contribute proxy loss. Sampled nodes add back the observed
oracle--proxy difference with inverse-propensity weight. With correct logged
propensities, \(\mathbb E[\ell_v^{\mathrm{corr}}\mid T]=\ell_v^\ast\).

The corrected local-law channel is
\[
  L_{\mathrm{law}}^{\mathrm{corr}}(g,T)
  =
  \sum_{v\in V(T)}\gamma^{d(v)}\ell_v^{\mathrm{corr}} .
\]
The finite-data objective is therefore
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:v8-corrected-objective}
  J_\lambda^{\mathrm{corr}}(g;T)
  =
  (1-\lambda)L_{\mathrm{root}}(g)
  +
  \lambda L_{\mathrm{law}}^{\mathrm{corr}}(g,T).
\end{equation}
The correction estimates the oracle local-law channel. It does not define a
new population target.

\subsection{HT Distortion Certificate}

For certificate transport, let \(\mathcal U\) be the finite population of
sample-eligible manifesto tree units, \(N=|\mathcal U|\), and let
\[
  D_i^J
  =
  d_Y\!\left(J(s_i),J(S(i))\right)
\]
be the distortion measured by an accessible judge or readout \(J\). If \(Z_i\)
is the audit inclusion indicator and \(\pi_i\) is the logged inclusion
probability, the Horvitz--Thompson estimator is
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:v8-ht-distortion}
  \widehat{\Delta}_{\mathrm{HT}}^{J}
  =
  \frac{1}{N}
  \sum_{i\in\mathcal U}
  \frac{Z_i}{\pi_i}D_i^J .
\end{equation}
With strict positivity and correct logged propensities, this is unbiased for
the corresponding finite-population mean. Unequal propensities affect variance
and effective sample size; they do not change the target being estimated.

The certificate combines the sampled distortion estimate with three envelopes:
calibration, sampling error, and clipping bias. In the compact reporting form,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:v8-finite-sample-certificate}
  |G_{\mathrm{meth}}|
  \le
  C_{\mathrm{meth}}\widehat{\Delta}_{\mathrm{HT}}
  +
  B_{\mathrm{cal}}
  +
  B_{\mathrm{est}}
  +
  B_{\mathrm{clip}} .
\end{equation}
Here \(B_{\mathrm{cal}}\) is the gap between the accessible judge and
\(f^\ast\), \(B_{\mathrm{est}}\) is sampling uncertainty after method
transport, and \(B_{\mathrm{clip}}\) is bias introduced by clipping
inverse-propensity weights or residuals. A high-probability version attaches
the corresponding failure budgets and combines them by a union bound.

\subsection{Reading the Manifesto Evidence}

The current Manifesto results use this stack in two tiers. The reported
held-out correlations are root-observed: the C-Tree root is scored against
external expert-survey means. The available local diagnostic figures are
proxy/teacher-trace diagnostics, which show where the local channel is likely
misaligned. A completed node-level certificate would sample realized leaves
and merges, evaluate them against an application-aligned \(f^\ast\) node
oracle, and report Equation~\eqref{eq:v8-finite-sample-certificate}.

Quasi-sentence codings provide the most direct Manifesto route to such an
audit. They are local policy statements already tied to the Manifesto coding
tradition. For a sampled leaf or merge span, those local statements can be
aggregated into a dimension-specific evidence target when the aggregation is
declared before the audit and matches the root policy dimension.
