\section{Scope and Related Work}\label{sec:v8-scope-related}

\subsection{Scope}

C-TreePO is scoped to long-context tasks where the target judgment is global,
the evidence can be represented through bounded local states, and a finite
population of leaf, state, and merge checks can be sampled with known
propensities. The framework separates three layers: representation through one
learned \(g\), root and local-law objectives through \(f^\ast\) and
\(\widehat f\), and design-based estimation of the local-law channel.

The guarantee is task-relative. C-TreePO does not claim generic semantic
compression, preservation of preferences outside the chosen oracle interface,
or certification of local laws without local oracle evidence. A root-observed
benchmark can validate document-level performance, as in
Section~\ref{sec:v8-manifesto-results}; a node-level local-law certificate is a
stronger and different measurement layer. When a downstream preference depends
on information outside the chosen oracle interface, the oracle or query family
must be expanded before the theorem applies.

For LLM measurement, the important distinction is root labels versus local
evidence labels. Root expert labels train and evaluate the final task. Local
proxy labels make the C-Tree trainable at scale. Sampled local oracle labels
identify proxy bias in the law channel. This is why the main math keeps both
\(f^\ast\) and \(\widehat f\) visible.

\subsection{Related Work}

The closest systems precedent is mergeable summaries for distributed and
streaming queries \citep{Agarwal2013MergeableTODS}. Database algebraic
aggregates \citep{GrayEtAl1997DataCube}, unordered MUD-style aggregation
\citep{FeldmanEtAl2008MUD}, and ordered homomorphism results
\citep{Gibbons1996ThirdHomomorphism} supply the state/merge/query template.
C-TreePO uses that template as an auditable target for learned semantic states.

The sampling layer is design-based supervised learning. Logged propensities
turn sampled local-law outcomes into HT estimates or Hajek-style
ratio-normalized estimates of the realized tree population, and clipping or
effective-sample-size diagnostics belong to that estimation layer. The
objective weights choose the root/local tradeoff; the audit estimates how well
the fitted tree satisfied the chosen target.

Neural operators and Fourier neural operators are treated as representation
families for state maps. Markov, HyperLogLog, and broader sketch comparisons
are appendix-side controls because their valid states are known or testable.
They serve as backstops for the same state-composition principle, while the
main text focuses on the LLM-side C-TreePO objective.
