\section{Conclusion}\label{sec:v8-conclusion}

C-TreePO treats manifesto measurement as a state-composition problem. The
expert score is global, but the evidence that determines it is local and
relational. A C-Tree stores policy-evidence states at leaves and internal
merges, and the local laws state when those states can replace the raw spans
they represent.

The objective separates the two channels a deployed LLM pipeline needs. Root
loss fits the document-level expert target. Local-law loss trains and audits
the state map inside the realized tree. Because direct local oracle calls are
sparse, the dense proxy \(\widehat f\) supplies local geometry and sampled
\(f^\ast\) nodes correct proxy bias with logged propensities.

The Manifesto/Benoit results demonstrate the root-observed tier: C-Tree roots
match strong expert-survey benchmarks while producing readable local units.
The decentralization failure mode shows why the local units matter. A root
score alone identifies the aggregate error; the local diagnostic gap points to
the state-sharing channel that failed. The next measurement tier is to sample
those realized nodes, attach application-aligned local oracle labels, and
report the finite-sample certificate alongside the root score.
