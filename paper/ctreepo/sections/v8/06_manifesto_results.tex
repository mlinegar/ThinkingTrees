\section{Manifesto Results: Main Application}
\label{sec:v8-manifesto-results}

The Manifesto application asks whether a learned C-Tree can preserve a
social-science measurement target while exposing readable local policy states.
The source documents are party election programs from the Manifesto Project.
The project releases document text through the Manifesto Corpus and
hand-coded policy-emphasis data through the Manifesto Data Collection
\citep{ManifestoProject2025a,ManifestoCorpus2025}. Its coding tradition goes
back to the Manifesto Research Group and Comparative Manifestos Project
\citep{BudgeRobertsonHearl1987,BudgeEtAl2001,KlingemannEtAl2006,VolkensEtAl2013};
\citet{MerzEtAl2016} describe the modern corpus as a multilingual text
resource. The unit of human coding is the quasi-sentence, a text unit
expressing one political statement and assigned to a policy category.

The benchmark organized by \citet{BenoitEtAl2025} matches \(235\) party
platforms to expert-survey means on six seven-point policy dimensions:
economic policy, social policy, immigration, European integration,
environmental policy, and decentralization. For each manifesto--dimension
pair, the target is the mean expert placement of the party on that issue
scale. Pearson \(r\) is the correlation between the root score and the expert
mean within a dimension. Macro \(r\) is the unweighted mean of the six
dimension correlations.

Benoit et al. also provide the empirical reference points used below. Their
split-expert row, macro \(r=0.873\), is a measurement-agreement reference
formed by correlating two expert groups. Their proprietary LLM ensemble reaches
macro \(r=0.817\), and the matched open-weight Gemma-3 replication reaches
macro \(r=0.793\). The C-Tree rows use the same policy targets while changing
the representation that is scored.

These results are a root-observed corpus evaluation. Each held-out
manifesto--dimension pair has a full-document expert target, and the tree root
is evaluated directly against that target. A node-level local-law audit is a
stronger tier: it would sample leaves, states, and merge spans with logged
propensities and compare their local evidence to an application-aligned
oracle. The current section reports the root-observed LLM application and the
available teacher-trace diagnostics.

\subsection{Tree Setup and Splits}\label{sec:v8-benoit-tree-setup}

The C-Tree version keeps Benoit's document-level target and changes the state
being scored. A flat baseline scores the manifesto text directly. A tree run
chunks the manifesto into contiguous leaves, maps each leaf into a readable
policy-evidence state, recursively merges adjacent states, and scores the
root state. The f/g ladder alternates the state map and readout: with
\(\widehat f\) fixed, train \(g\) against node targets; with \(g\) fixed,
train \(\widehat f\) on the summaries produced by the current tree. A ladder
stage such as \(f^2g^1\) means the second readout update paired with the first
state-map update.

The current f/g artifacts use \(218\) manifestos split into \(140\) training
documents, \(30\) reserved documents used for model selection and diagnostics,
and \(48\) held-out test documents used for final reporting. Each manifesto is
materialized as one tree for each leaf size. The node count changes with leaf
size; smaller leaves create more audit and training units, while larger leaves
move more evidence into each local state. Reported metrics are held-out root
Pearson correlations against expert-survey means. The generated figures and
tables live under \texttt{assets/benoit/}. Appendix~\ref{app:v8-benoit-replication}
records the corpus, split, benchmark, control, and artifact provenance details.

\subsection{Single-Dimension Results}\label{sec:v8-benoit-single-dim}

Single-dimension training uses one \((\widehat f,g)\) pair per policy
dimension. This is the per-dimension ceiling for the tree representation
before asking one shared summary to serve all six dimensions.

  \caption{\textbf{Single-dimension f/g results, all six policy dimensions.}
  External Pearson \(r\) against expert-survey means on the \(48\)-document
  held-out split, plotted against leaf token size. External means the score is
  computed on documents not used for training or model selection. Reference
  lines are from \citet{BenoitEtAl2025}.}
  \label{fig:v8-benoit-singledim-per-dim}
\end{figure}

Figure~\ref{fig:v8-benoit-singledim-per-dim} resolves the per-dimension
ceiling. Economic policy, EU integration, immigration, and social policy clear
the proprietary ensemble reference in their best cells. Environment remains
near the open-weight reference. Decentralization is the stress dimension: it
improves under single-dimension training and can clear the proprietary
ensemble, but it remains well below split-expert agreement and its local
diagnostic gap stays high.

The decentralization pattern is informative. The dimension asks the state to
retain institutional distinctions that vary by country and by policy area:
administrative devolution, fiscal autonomy, regional identity, veto power, and
central budget authority. A single scalar local score cannot carry these
distinctions to the parent. Even a readable state can blur them unless the
state map is trained for that dimension directly.

\subsection{Universal Summary}\label{sec:v8-benoit-universal-summary}

The universal-summary run trains one shared \(g\) to produce a single summary
per manifesto. Six per-dimension scorers then read that shared state. This is
the LLM application closest to the C-TreePO objective: root expert anchors
preserve the document target, while teacher/proxy local-law losses push one
state map to retain evidence for multiple downstream readouts.

\subsection{Local Diagnostics and Node-Label Route}
\label{sec:v8-benoit-local-diagnostics}

The available local diagnostics are teacher-trace residuals, not a completed
human node audit. A teacher-trace residual is the discrepancy between a
recorded teacher local target and the current proxy/readout behavior at the
corresponding node. These diagnostics still serve the v8 objective story: the
proxy \(\widehat f\) produces local-law losses everywhere, while sampled
\(f^\ast\) node calls can correct the proxy channel using
Equation~\eqref{eq:v8-corrected-node-loss}.

The local diagnostic figures identify where proxy supervision is least aligned
with the held-out expert target. Under the v8 objective, those are the nodes
where sampled \(f^\ast\) labels have the highest value: they estimate and
correct \(b_v=\widehat\ell_v-\ell_v^\ast\) rather than merely adding another
root score.

The Manifesto Project quasi-sentence codings provide the natural path from
diagnostics to human node audit. For sampled leaf or merge spans, the relevant
quasi-sentences can be aggregated into policy-dimension evidence targets and
compared with the produced state. Root expert means continue to calibrate the
document-level target; sampled node evidence supplies the local-law channel
used by Equation~\eqref{eq:v8-finite-sample-certificate}.

\subsection{Takeaways}\label{sec:v8-benoit-takeaway}

The Manifesto result reads as an auditable-compression result. The
per-dimension tree reaches the published frontier range at the root, and the
universal-summary tree remains close on five of six dimensions. Every run also
produces a finite population of leaves, summaries, and merge states.
Decentralization is the dimension where the universal-summary run breaks, and
the local diagnostic gap flags the breakdown without needing to inspect the
root score alone. The stronger measurement layer is to turn those diagnostic
units into a sampled node-level oracle audit.
