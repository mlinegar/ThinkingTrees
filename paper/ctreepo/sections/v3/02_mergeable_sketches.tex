\section{Mergeable Sketches and the Algebraic Target}\label{sec:mergeable-sketches}

C-TreePO inherits its shape from a familiar engineering pattern. Database
and streaming systems have long handled aggregate queries by splitting a
stream into shards, building a compact summary of each shard, merging
sibling summaries pairwise up a tree, and reading the query off the root.
HyperLogLog estimates distinct counts in a few kilobytes, Count-Min
answers frequency queries under a space budget, Bloom filters answer
membership; in each, the summary state, the merge operator, and the
readout are engineered \emph{before} training, and correctness is proved
once by algebra rather than checked at runtime
\citep{Agarwal2013MergeableTODS,FlajoletEtAl2007,CormodeMuthukrishnan2005}.
C-TreePO generalizes the same pattern to settings where no hand-designed
sketch is available (a manifesto, a clinical narrative, a play) by
replacing the hand-designed merge with a learned summarizer $g$ and
replacing algebraic correctness with a sampled audit at the leaves and
merges. Table~\ref{tab:sketch-mapping} makes the
correspondence concrete: every column of a classical mergeable sketch
has a C-TreePO counterpart, with the runtime audit standing in for the
hand-designed merge's algebraic proof.

\paragraph{Fibers and sufficient states.}
A \emph{state} for the oracle $\fstar$ is any map $\sigma:X\to S$
whose value determines the oracle: if two inputs $x,x'$ are assigned
the same state, $\fstar$ does not distinguish them. Calling the
preimage $\sigma^{-1}(s)=\{x\in X:\sigma(x)=s\}$ the \emph{fiber} of
$\sigma$ over $s$, the condition is that every $\sigma$-fiber lies
inside a single $\fstar$-fiber:
\[
    \sigma(x)=\sigma(x') \quad\Longrightarrow\quad
    \fstar(x)=\fstar(x').
\]
Equivalently, there is a readout $F:S\to Y$ with
$\fstar(x)=F(\sigma(x))$: once $\sigma(x)$ is known, raw-text
differences inside the same fiber are irrelevant to the oracle. This
is the standard sufficiency idea
\citep{Fisher1922,Blackwell1953,Kallenberg2002FMP}, specialized to an
oracle-valued text target. The mergeable-sketch requirement is one
further algebraic condition: the state itself composes locally,
$\sigma(x\concat y)=\mathrm{merge}(\sigma(x),\sigma(y))$, so the root
state can be recovered from leaf states without rereading raw text
\citep{Agarwal2013MergeableTODS}.

The summarizer $g$ produces an estimate of $\sigma(x)$ in two
practical forms. When $g$ is a language model
(Section~\ref{sec:manifesto-llm}), the estimate $g(x)$ is a
plain-text \emph{summary}: a paragraph or page of natural language
that stands in for the state the rubric oracle would need. When $g$
is a neural operator or an embedding model
(Section~\ref{sec:fno-primer}), the estimate is a vector that we
assume approximates $\sigma(x)$ up to a tolerance the local-law audit
measures. The fiber language describes what each realization is
trying to capture; the local laws (Section~\ref{sec:framework}) and
the audit (Section~\ref{sec:estimation}) develop what it means for
an estimate to pass.

Section~\ref{sec:framework} develops the local laws that make the
audit precise, Section~\ref{sec:theorems} the optimization view that
makes them trainable, and Section~\ref{sec:estimation} the gap bound
that makes them reportable. The classical-to-
learned correspondence is also a formal claim:
Proposition~\ref{prop:mergeable-reduction} (stated and proved in
Appendix~\ref{app:mergeable-background}) shows that when $g$ serializes
a classical mergeable sketch, C-TreePO reduces to that sketch under the
local laws: byte-for-byte for register-based implementations, and in
expectation otherwise. Classical full mergeable summaries nest in C-TreePO
through relational state-level C-Trees. Randomized mergeable summaries nest in
the same formulation in probability: root readout is correct on the event that
the merged root state is valid. This keeps Agarwal et al.'s union view intact:
shards combine as datasets, $\varepsilon$ remains fixed in the size/error
profile, and sample-size or training-data requirements enter as separate
concentration side conditions rather than scalar child-query merge laws.
