\section{Introduction}\label{sec:min-introduction}

The motivating problem is a single social-science measurement task. A party manifesto is a long document, and a researcher wants one
economic-policy score: where does the party sit on the public-services versus
taxation dimension used in the Manifesto/Benoit benchmark
\citep{BenoitEtAl2025,ManifestoProject2025a}? The score is document-level,
but the evidence is not. Fiscal pledges, welfare commitments, tax positions,
industrial policy, and the qualifications that govern how any of those
statements should be read are spread across paragraphs and sections. The
useful question is therefore whether we can work at a smaller granularity
than the whole manifesto without changing the economic-position target.

C-TreePO treats that question as a local state-composition problem. We
assume there is a task-relative economic state $S_{\mathrm{econ}}(x)$ for any
span $x$: all evidence in that span that a trusted economic-policy coder
would need for the chosen rubric, and nothing of the slogans or style that
cannot change the final score. The state is conceptual rather than directly
observed---the theorems of Section~\ref{sec:min-guarantees} treat
$S_{\mathrm{econ}}$, its readout $r_{\mathrm{econ}}$, and the implied
target
$\fstar_{\mathrm{econ}}(x) = r_{\mathrm{econ}}\!\left(S_{\mathrm{econ}}(x)\right)$
as assumptions about the measurement problem rather than claims about
political text. A language-model summary, a hand-coded quasi-sentence
aggregate, or a learned vector state is only a surrogate for that state. A
compression tree is valid when those surrogates preserve the oracle-visible
economic state as spans are summarized and merged;
Section~\ref{sec:min-manifesto-state} makes that precise on the example, and
Section~\ref{sec:min-framework} writes it down as three local laws.

The practical promise is granularity reduction. If a paragraph- or
section-level summary preserves the economic evidence in that span, and if
merging sibling summaries preserves the interaction visible in their union,
then a researcher can label, audit, and optimize many smaller units whenever
the application calls for node-level certification or label-budget
substitution. That promise sits alongside---not instead of---the
Manifesto/Benoit evaluation reported in
Section~\ref{sec:min-manifesto-results}. That evaluation is
\emph{root-observed}: every sampled platform already carries a full-document
expert target, and the tree root is scored directly against it on the
benchmark corpus. It is a complete claim at its own tier. A node-level audit
of preservation distortion, and a local-label training path that substitutes
paragraph- or section-level targets for root labels, are stronger tiers on
top of it, not prerequisites under it. Local labels pay only when they
measure the same oracle-preservation property the root task cares about---
Markov changepoints in Section~\ref{sec:min-validation} are the clean
evidence that they can, and the Manifesto quasi-sentence codings are the
planned route for this setting. When local checks fail on a deployment that
claims a node-level certificate, the audit estimates the resulting
oracle distortion rather than burying the failure as an unobserved
engineering step.

The paper follows this one example first.
Section~\ref{sec:min-manifesto-state} defines the economic state, its
readout, and the sense in which a summary can preserve it.
Section~\ref{sec:min-framework} turns that setup into a compression tree and
three local laws. Section~\ref{sec:min-guarantees} states the
root-preservation, preference-alignment, and finite-sample-certificate
guarantees in compact form. Section~\ref{sec:min-manifesto-results} reports
the Manifesto/Benoit headline and its leaf-size robustness.
Section~\ref{sec:min-validation} returns to Markov changepoints and
HyperLogLog as mechanism tests where the state is already known: Markov tests
ordered boundary state, and HLL tests state-level mergeability with an
irreducible sketch noise floor. Section~\ref{sec:min-backstop} carries the
algebraic and representation backstop that ties the framework to classical
mergeable-sketch theory, and Section~\ref{sec:min-audit} describes the
label-budget audit that converts approximate preservation into a
reportable certificate. Section~\ref{sec:min-applications} closes on where
the framework applies and where it fails. The appendices carry
the proof map, the neural-operator realization, and extended empirics.
