\section{Conclusion}\label{sec:min-conclusion}

Long-document compression should preserve a task-relative state, not
merely produce a fluent shorter text. For Manifesto
economic measurement, the assumed state is the economic-policy evidence a
trusted rubric oracle would need. C-TreePO makes that state-composition claim
local: leaves preserve span evidence, merges preserve union-span interactions,
and re-summarization does not drift.

That local structure is useful because it changes the granularity of the work.
When the laws hold, a researcher can sample, label, and audit paragraphs,
sections, or chapters while preserving the full-manifesto economic target.
When they do not hold, the audit turns the failure into a measured gap. The
Manifesto/Benoit result is the root-observed case: full-document expert
labels are available for the sampled corpus, and the tree root is scored
against them. Markov and HLL show the same logic in controlled and classical
limits; the Manifesto local-law certificate is the stronger node-level path
where the economic state is learned or prompted and then checked inside the
tree.
