\section{Conclusion}\label{sec:conclusion}

The main claim of C-TreePO is simple: if local summaries preserve the evidence a task oracle needs, and if local merges preserve the interactions that become visible only after spans are combined, then long-document preference optimization can be carried out on compression trees rather than on full documents. The paper's theorem ladder makes that claim explicit: the structural bundle gives root preservation, the optimization bundle gives summary-based objective equivalence, and the certificate bundle turns sampled audits into a finite-sample bound on objective drift.

This paper is intentionally about the method core rather than the systems layer. It is strongest for tasks with well-defined content-based oracles on bounded spans, and weaker for tasks driven by style, rhetoric, or other context effects that fall outside the oracle. In those settings the audits are still useful because they show \emph{how} the compression pipeline fails, but the strongest equivalence claims no longer apply. The Semantic Forests companion focuses on orchestration and deployment at system scale; C-TreePO provides the certification layer that those larger systems need.
