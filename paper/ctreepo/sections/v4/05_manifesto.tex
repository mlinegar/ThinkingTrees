\section{Manifesto Results: Main Application}\label{sec:min-manifesto-results}

The benchmark covers $235$ party platforms with expert-survey means on
six seven-point policy dimensions. We run each platform through the
C-tree pipeline of Section~\ref{sec:min-framework} and correlate the
root score against the expert-survey mean for each dimension. The
evaluation is root-observed: every sampled platform comes with a
full-document target. The node-level audit path of
Section~\ref{sec:min-audit} returns in
Section~\ref{sec:min-node-supervision}.

Macro correlation stays in a $0.027$-wide band across the full

Schedule invariance (Corollary~\ref{cor:schedule}) is the formal claim
with leaves held fixed; the empirical numbers extend it across leaf
sizes, so the choice of how finely to split the manifesto becomes an
audit-budget question (smaller leaves give more sampling sites) without
changing the document-level estimand.

The headline runs the per-dimension pipeline (one summarizer and one
scorer per dimension). The same table reports a combined pipeline,
which uses one shared summarizer for all six dimensions and a joint
scorer against the resulting summary, at macro $0.78$--$0.81$ across
the matched leaf sweep ($\sim\!0.02$ below the per-dimension band).
Section~\ref{sec:min-fg-ladder} takes both pipelines under prompt
training and shows where the combined variant fails. Compute and

\subsection[Alternating f/g Optimization]{Alternating $f/g$ Optimization}\label{sec:min-fg-ladder}

The alternating $f/g$ prompt ladder applies the framework's alternation
principle to the prompts themselves. Hold the summarizer/merge prompt
$g$ fixed, optimize the scorer prompt $f$ against the current
summaries; swap roles and optimize $g$ against the current scorer;
repeat. The compact label $f^a g^b$ marks the scorer after $a$
scorer-side rounds and the summarizer after $b$ summarizer-side rounds.
Each cell reports two correlations on the held-out split: external
Pearson against expert-survey means (the gold target) and internal
Pearson against the previous stage's scorer (a self-consistency check).
The $f^*$-gap is the difference between the two. A small gap means the
ladder converges to a scorer that agrees with the previous scorer and
the gold labels at once. A large gap means the teacher-vs-gold
disagreement caps what training-against-teacher alone can reach, and
the ladder's external Pearson is bounded below the teacher's internal
Pearson by the size of that disagreement.\footnote{The ladder accepts
several initial conditions for $g^0$ and $f^1$ (Benoit's archived
GPT-4o summaries with a scorer-only optimized prompt; an own-Gemma
raw-init pair; identity initialization). The init scheme is an
engineering detail; results below report whichever cell is available
for each lane. Full grids appear in Appendix~\ref{app:min-manifesto}.}

The Economics ladder gives the cleanest reading on a dimension where
the teacher carries most of the signal. Per-leaf best external Pearson
sits at $0.885$ (leaf $1024$), $0.886$ ($2048$), $0.886$ ($4096$), and
$0.879$ ($8192$): a $0.007$-wide band straddling the economic
split-expert agreement reference of $0.880$ across an $8\times$ leaf
range. Below roughly $1024$ tokens the ladder degrades gracefully
($0.861$ at leaf $512$, $0.830$ at $256$); leaves too small to carry
the evidence a later merge needs. The $f^*$-gap stays in
$[0.086, 0.161]$ across the populated grid, with internal Pearson in
$[0.953, 0.990]$. The ladder co-adapts $f$ and $g$ without racing
ahead of gold, and the teacher carries the dimension to within
$0.10$--$0.16$ of the split-expert ceiling.

The Decentralization ladder gives the same shape on a much harder
axis. At leaf $256$ tokens, the trajectory is
$0.534 \to 0.557 \to 0.557$ across $f^1g^0 \to f^1g^1 \to f^2g^1$. One
$g$-update lifts the correlation by $0.023$; the next $f$-update lands
on the same fixed point to four decimals; the ladder converges in one
$g/f$ cycle. The Pearson sits above the proprietary 18-score ensemble
of \citet{BenoitEtAl2025} on this dimension ($0.490$) and at parity
with the per-dimension pipeline at the $\sim\!2\mathrm{K}$-token leaf
row (Table~\ref{tab:min-benoit-headline}, $0.543$) at roughly $1/8$
the leaf budget. The $f^*$-gap is bounded ($0.42 \to 0.40 \to 0.40$,
internal Pearson in $[0.951, 0.960]$) and moves cleanly through the
cycle the way it moves on Economics. Its absolute level is the
load-bearing fact: $\sim\!0.40$, four times the Economics gap. The
diagnostic flags decentralization as teacher-bounded; the scorer
matches the teacher very well, and the teacher carries the remaining
gap to gold.%

The centralized-$g$ configuration applies the same ladder under a
shared summarizer. One $g$ produces a single summary for each
manifesto, and one joint scorer (the
\texttt{JointDimensionScorer} of \S\ref{sec:min-framework}) returns
six per-dimension scores against that summary. The $g$-training metric
is the unweighted mean of per-dimension $f$-rewards; the $f$-training
step runs the same averaged metric on the scoring side. Six leaf
rungs ($256$--$8096$ tokens) and six alternating stages ($f^1g^0$
through $f^3g^3$) populate the grid.

Five of six dimensions track each other across the centralized-$g$
ladder. At $f^1g^0$ across the $32\times$ leaf sweep, the
per-dimension external Pearson bands are $0.014$ (immigration), $0.017$
(EU), $0.025$ (social), $0.030$ (economic), and $0.045$ (environment).
All five sit in bands narrower than the $0.027$-wide macro band of
\S\ref{sec:min-headline}, so the leaf-size invariance of the headline
extends from per-dimension training into centralized-$g$ alternating
optimization. The $f^*$-gap on these five sits in $[0.03, 0.16]$, the
same regime the per-dimension Economics ladder reports. Decentralization
sits in $0.30$--$0.39$ across all leaf sizes and its $f^*$-gap is
$0.45$, three to five times higher than the other five. Bigger leaves
do not help. The diagnostic that read cleanly on the per-dimension
ladders flags decentralization as the failure mode under centralized
$g$.

At leaf $8096$, the centralized-$g$ trajectory on decentralization
oscillates: $0.361 \to 0.461 \to 0.343 \to 0.343 \to 0.359 \to 0.413$
across $f^1g^0$ through $f^3g^3$. One $g$-step lifts decentralization
by $0.10$, the largest single-step gain anywhere in the grid; the next
$f$-step pulls it back below the $f^1g^0$ baseline; the trajectory
oscillates without converging. The mechanism is multi-task interference
on the $f$ side. The $f$-gradient runs on the averaged six-dimension
metric, so a dimension whose latent direction is far from the consensus
receives $1/6$ of the gradient signal, and every $g$-step that lifts
it gets unwound on the next $f$-step. The compression diagnosis sits
on the $f$ side. The $g$ output budget is $2\times$ leaf-size, and
the $f^1g^1$ result shows $g$ can encode decentralization within that
budget when the reward signal points there.

The single-target ladder of \S\ref{sec:min-fg-perdim} removes the
averaging, and the pullback goes with it. Its $f^2g^1$ rung lands at
the $f^1g^1$ fixed point to four decimals, while the elevated
$f^*$-gap inherited from the dimension's teacher-bounded character
stays bounded through the cycle. What remains is the teacher ceiling.
Per-dimension ladders are the right default. Centralized $g$ works
whenever the $f^*$-gap on every dimension stays in the band the
per-dimension ladders show, and falls back to per-dimension training
when any single axis pushes the gap outside that band.

\subsection{Toward Node-Level Supervision}\label{sec:min-node-supervision}

The current results are root-observed. The leaf-size invariance is
empirical evidence that the algebra's prediction holds on this corpus,
but the audit machinery of Section~\ref{sec:min-audit} has not yet been
exercised against node-level oracle labels. The Manifesto Project's
expert quasi-sentence codings make node-level supervision available
without any new annotation budget: $2{,}157$ platforms cover
$\sim\!2.27$M coded spans, and aggregating those codings inside any leaf
or merge span gives a ground-truth oracle value at that node. That is
the same kind of design-based partial-document supervision the audit was
built to consume, applied to training rather than to certification.

The implementation that wires those codings into a node-oracle cache and
into the C-TreePO trainer's local-law supervision channel is in place;
oracle-grounded training results will be added on convergence and
reported in Appendix~\ref{app:min-manifesto}. The forward-looking
distillation chain of Section~\ref{sec:min-discussion} (gold human
labels $\to$ strong-LLM proxy $\to$ small-LM student with node-level
labels) is the next-tier extension of the same recipe.

\subsection{Preference Optimization on Tree Summaries}\label{sec:min-preference-app}

Theorem~\ref{thm:pref-equiv} makes the headline operationally useful
beyond correlation. When downstream preference training targets the same
oracle (\emph{which summary better captures the manifesto's position on
dimension $d$?}), preferences can be collected on root summaries rather
than on full documents. Under local preservation and the
context-compatibility condition of Assumption~\ref{ass:context}, the
summary-based and document-based DPO and GRPO population minimizers
coincide; under approximate preservation,
Theorem~\ref{thm:e2e} converts the realized-violation estimate
$\hat{\Delta}_R$ into a quantitative drift envelope on the training
objective. The application-level premise is that human preferences
depend on the manifesto only through the preserved oracle value (and not
on tone, register, or formatting). When that premise fails, the residual
is carried through the same calibration term $B_{\mathrm{cal}}$ used in
the certificate; the framework reports the failure rather than absorbing
it.
