# C-TreePO Paper Handoff — 2026-04-16

Quick status writeup for the next LLM or collaborator picking up this work.

## What this paper is

C-TreePO: a framework for compressing long documents into hierarchical summaries while preserving a task oracle. The paper is built around three "local laws" (Sufficiency / C1, Idempotence / C2, Merge Consistency / C3) and ladders from structural preservation → preference-objective equivalence (DPO/GRPO) → finite-sample audit certificates.

**Target audience**: political / social scientists and ML researchers who work with long documents (manifestos, legal records, clinical narratives). The running example is Markov changepoint color-transition counting on synthetic documents, which builds up to semantic tasks like manifesto RILE scoring.

**Current length**: 35 pages, compiled and clean (no undefined references).

## Section 3 structure (the example)

1. **Setup** — color-transition DGP, 4 or 12 colors, synonym tokens, topic-change counting
2. **Models** — flat FNO vs tree: same FNO backbone, different scaffolding. Training in 2 stages.
3. **Experimental Setup** — DGP table (`tab:dgp`) + tree geometry sub-table + budget accounting
4. **Results** — three figures (walkthrough, simple replacement view, hard replacement view, budget-split view) + three findings + one bridge paragraph

**Key framing**: budget is counted in **token-observations**, not abstract "mass." A root label = $1.0$ unit (all 128 tokens); a leaf label on $\ell$ tokens = $\ell/128$ units. The reallocation policies keep total token budget fixed at the $100\%$ level and split between root and local labels.

## Key narrative points the paper makes

1. **Decomposition does not lose information** — tree matches/beats flat FNO at every supervision level, confirmed by one-leaf parity canary.
2. **The supervision budget can be split** — replacing root labels with local labels of equivalent token mass barely affects root accuracy, and the allocation policy (leaf-only, depth-equal, balanced-node) barely matters.
3. **Limits are real** — hard case compresses margins; very low root budgets degrade all policies; policy divergence sharper at low budgets.

Closing bridge: the same mechanism applies to semantic tasks (RILE scoring, rubric-driven extraction); only the sufficient statistic and merge rule get richer.

## Key notes

- **Model is "unified_g"**: the merge IS a learned neural network (merge projector → shared encoder $g$), NOT a hard-coded algebraic rule. An earlier description was wrong about this; current text is correct.
- **Training is two-stage**: stage 1 = local laws only (10 epochs), stage 2 = root loss at 20% + local at 80% (30 epochs). Flat FNO = single stage, root loss only, 40 epochs.
- **Architecture details are in footnotes** — main text says "same FNO backbone, different scaffolding."
