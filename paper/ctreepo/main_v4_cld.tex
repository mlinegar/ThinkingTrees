\title{C-TreePO: An Algebra and Audit for LLM-Based Long-Document Measurement}
\author{Mitchell Linegar}
\date{}

\begin{document}
\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
Two facts are well known: LLMs carry their own (sometimes substantial)
biases on text of any length, and even small biases in LLM-produced
labels turn into large biases in downstream regression estimates. Long
documents amplify both problems, and document-level gold-standard
labels that could correct the gap are expensive. We show that compression trees (or C-trees) preserve the oracle value
of a full document at the root for a large class of oracle judgments
and preferences, under three local laws stated on the leaves and
merges. C-TreePO (Compression-Tree Preference Optimization) pairs that
algebra with a design-based audit
(inverse-propensity weighting over sampled leaves, stored summaries,
and merge nodes) that returns a finite-sample envelope on the
downstream estimand when the laws hold only approximately. With an
additional oracle-factorization assumption, algorithms such as DPO and
GRPO trained on root summaries target the same population minimum as
trained on full documents. Cheap node-level labels and expensive document-level
gold-standard labels feed the same shrinking envelope, so a
researcher can reallocate gold-standard supervision across the tree
without changing the estimand. The object of supervision becomes a design choice: the
researcher picks the unit of measurement that gives the most signal
per unit of expert effort. On the Manifesto Project (a long-document corpus with
gold-standard expert annotations), a single open-weight C-tree pipeline
equals or outperforms an ensemble of frontier proprietary LLMs
\citep{BenoitEtAl2025} across six policy dimensions. The framework treats summaries as approximate
sufficient statistics on a tree merge, extending the mergeable-sketch
algebra \citep{Agarwal2013MergeableTODS,HehirTingCormode2023SFM} to
learned, observable summaries audited at runtime; simulations on
synthetic settings (where the global oracle is known by construction)
confirm that the local structure lets us collect gold labels at any
level of the tree without changing the estimand.
\end{abstract}
