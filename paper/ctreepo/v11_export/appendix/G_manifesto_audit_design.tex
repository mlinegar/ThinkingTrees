\section{Manifesto Node-Audit Design}
\label{app:v8-manifesto-audit-design}

This appendix gives a concrete audit design for the stronger Manifesto claim
tier. It is not needed to read the root-observed results in
Section~\ref{sec:v8-manifesto-results}; it specifies how those results become
a node-level local-law certificate.

\subsection{Audit Population}

For each held-out manifesto and policy dimension, materialize the same C-Tree
used for the root score. The finite population contains three unit types:
\[
  \mathcal U
  =
  \mathcal U_{\mathrm{leaf}}
  \cup
  \mathcal U_{\mathrm{state}}
  \cup
  \mathcal U_{\mathrm{merge}} .
\]
Leaf units test C1, state units test C2, and merge units test C3. Each unit
stores the document identifier, policy dimension, tree topology, leaf size,
node depth, represented raw span, produced state, active law channel, and
logged inclusion probability.

The represented raw span is part of the audit record. For leaves it is the
original manifesto text. For internal nodes it is the concatenation of the
descendant leaves. For state units it is the span represented by the state that
is being re-entered into the state map. This bookkeeping prevents local
diagnostics from drifting away from the finite-population target.

\subsection{Manifesto Local Oracle}

The local oracle must measure the same policy dimension as the root target.
For expert-survey dimensions, the practical candidates are:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.5em]
  \item expert or high-quality teacher judgments over sampled spans,
  \item quasi-sentence codings aggregated into dimension-specific evidence
        targets, and
  \item hybrid labels where a teacher proposes the local policy evidence and a
        human verifies the dimension-specific content.
\end{enumerate}
The aggregation rule must be declared before the audit. For example, an
economic-policy leaf target can aggregate quasi-sentences related to taxation,
public services, welfare, privatization, labor-market policy, and fiscal
constraints. A decentralization target needs a different rule, separating
administrative devolution, fiscal autonomy, federal reform, and regional
authority.

The local oracle can be categorical, scalar, or structured. C-TreePO only
requires a declared discrepancy \(d_Y\) for comparing the state-side readout
with the span-side target. A structured local target is often preferable for
manifestos because it preserves both why a span matters and where it falls on
a scale.

\subsection{Sampling Design}

A simple design samples within strata defined by policy dimension, law channel,
node depth, and diagnostic-risk bucket. Diagnostic risk can be based on the
teacher-trace gap from the current pipeline, with a positive floor in every
stratum. The positive floor is important: every audit unit needs
\(\pi_i>0\) for HT estimation.

The design should log marginal propensities rather than only sample counts.
For a unit \(i\), the audit record stores \(\pi_i\), any clipping cap applied
to \(1/\pi_i\), and the stratum that generated it. If sampling is adaptive, the
logged propensity is conditional on the information available before the draw.
The HT estimator remains targeted at the realized finite population when those
logged propensities are correct.

For reporting, include the raw sampled count, weighted effective sample size,
minimum inclusion probability, maximum unclipped weight, and any clipping cap
for each law channel. These quantities make the variance side of the
certificate interpretable.

\subsection{Law-Specific Checks}

C1 checks compare a leaf state with the raw leaf span. In the Manifesto
setting, this asks whether the produced policy-evidence state contains the
dimension-relevant claims in the span. A C1 failure occurs when the state drops
or changes an evidence item that can move the expert score.

C2 checks compare a produced state with its re-summarized version. This asks
whether an already compressed policy state is stable under reuse. A C2 failure
occurs when a conditional claim becomes unconditional, a country-specific
institutional claim is generalized away, or a state becomes more extreme or
more moderate without new evidence.

C3 checks compare a merged state with the raw parent span represented by the
two children. This is the key cross-section check. A C3 failure occurs when
the merge preserves each child in isolation but loses the relation between
them, such as a tax pledge qualified by a later fiscal rule, a green investment
pledge limited by energy-security exceptions, or a decentralization claim that
separates administrative authority from fiscal authority.

\subsection{Report}

A node-level Manifesto audit should report:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.5em]
  \item root-observed performance against expert-survey means,
  \item per-law violation rates for C1, C2, and C3,
  \item HT distortion estimates by law channel and overall,
  \item calibration, estimation, and clipping envelopes,
  \item diagnostic slices by dimension, especially decentralization, and
  \item examples of sampled failures with raw span, produced state, and
        local-oracle target.
\end{enumerate}
The examples are not part of the statistical estimator, but they make the
failure modes auditable by domain experts. In manifesto measurement, that
interpretability is a practical advantage of text states over opaque scalar
or vector-only compression.
