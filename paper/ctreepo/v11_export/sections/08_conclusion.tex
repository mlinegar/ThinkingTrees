\section{Conclusion}\label{sec:v8-conclusion}

C-TreePO treats long-document measurement as a state-composition
problem. The expert score is global; the evidence that determines
it is local and relational. A C-Tree stores evidence states at the
leaves and at every internal merge, and the local laws say when
those states can replace the raw spans they represent. The objective
separates two channels: a root loss fits the document-level expert
target, and a local-law channel trains and audits the state map
inside the realized tree. Sparse oracle calls and logged
propensities correct proxy bias in the local channel.

The Manifesto LLM replication gives us the root-observed tier. The
per-dimension tree reaches macro \(r=0.829\); the universal-summary
configuration reaches \(0.807\). Five of the six policy dimensions
track their per-dimension ceilings under shared compression.
Decentralization is where shared compression breaks down, and the
local diagnostic gap flags that breakdown directly, without any
appeal to root-score comparisons. The Markov sandbox fixes the
oracle and sufficient state, then shows how local labels can
replace some root-label mass under a fixed budget when they
supervise the state needed for composition.

\paragraph{Scope.}
C-TreePO is scoped to rubric-driven long-document measurement
where the target judgment is global, evidence appears in local
spans, and a finite population of leaf, state, and merge checks
can be sampled with known propensities. Party manifestos are the
motivating case. The same structure can arise in policy platforms,
legislative records, clinical narratives, and book-length reports,
provided the target can be expressed as a task-relative state that
survives local replacement. The guarantee is task-relative: a
C-Tree trained to preserve economic-policy evidence does not
automatically preserve rhetoric, coalition strategy, or writing
style. Those quantities require either a wider oracle or a separate
measurement channel.

\paragraph{Deployment guidance.}
A deployment should declare the measurement target first: a
dimension, a scale, and an expert oracle. The state rubric, root
scorer, local labels, and calibration set should all be written
against that target. Run the root-observed benchmark and the local
diagnostics together. Root correlation says whether the final
score is useful; local gaps say where the mechanism needs audit.
If the root score is strong and local gaps are small, the
certificate can focus on sampling enough nodes to tighten
\(B_{\mathrm{est}}\). If the root score is strong but local gaps
are large, the right response is targeted node labeling, not
celebration of the root number. If both root and local evidence
are weak, the state map or target rubric needs redesign.

The reported result should include the tier of evidence. A
root-observed benchmark supports a claim about held-out document
scores. A teacher-trace diagnostic supports a claim about proxy
behavior. A sampled node-level certificate supports a claim about
realized local-law distortion. Those tiers can reinforce one
another, but they should not be collapsed. The point of C-TreePO
is to make the measurement stack inspectable enough that any
consumer of the result can tell which claim has actually been
earned.
