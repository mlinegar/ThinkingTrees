# C-TreePO: Design-Based Partial Document Supervision for Long-Document Political Measurement

Political methodologists increasingly use large language models to turn long texts into measurements, but once documents exceed context limits the research design itself changes: the analyst no longer observes the full text, only a compressed surrogate produced by truncation, retrieval, or recursive summarization. Design-based Supervised Learning (DSL), especially Egami et al. (2024), shows that naively substituting cheap surrogate labels for gold-standard labels can bias downstream inference even when predictive accuracy appears strong, yet existing DSL-style work largely treats the surrogate as a predicted label rather than a compressed representation. We introduce C-TreePO, an auditable compression-tree framework that treats long-document coding as an oracle-preserving measurement problem: documents are partitioned into spans, summarized up a tree, and checked locally for sufficiency, idempotence, and merge consistency so that compression-induced measurement error can be estimated rather than assumed away. We demonstrate the approach on Manifesto Project RILE scoring under partial document supervision, where only sampled substructures receive additional oracle attention while document-level ideological judgments remain tied to the full-document target. The broader contribution is methodological: C-TreePO extends the design-based logic of DSL to long political texts by making compression, auditing, and logged supervision part of the measurement design rather than hidden preprocessing choices.
